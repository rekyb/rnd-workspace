# Notes — Uxcel (analysis)

Source: Mobbin reference library, `web`, accessed 2026-07-29. Three screens. **Reference-library
observation, not first-party.**

## Observation 1 — thirteen destinations, and it reads as three (Q1)

This is the study's most directly useful Q1 result, because **Uxcel carries more destinations
than we do — thirteen against our eleven — and does not look crowded.**

The difference is entirely grouping:

- **3 ungrouped, top:** `Home`, `Bookmarks`, `Leagues`
- **`LEARN` (6):** `Courses`, `Career Paths`, `Briefs`, `Assessments`, `Tutorials`, `Arcade`
- **`GROW` (4):** `Showcase`, `Certifications`, `Salary Explorer`, `Job Board`

Three chunks of 3, 6, and 4, each scannable, against our undifferentiated run of eleven.

The grouping rule is about **what the destination is for**: `LEARN` items build skill, `GROW`
items convert skill into work or recognition. That is a learner-meaningful distinction, not an
internal one — a learner who wants a job goes to `GROW` without reading every label.

Set beside the others, the study now holds four distinct published answers to the same problem:

| Platform | Destinations | Device |
|---|---|---|
| Babbel | 3 | Keep the top level small; push depth down |
| Coursera | 4 learner tabs | Same, with a separate global bar |
| Duolingo | 8 + `MORE` | Budget the visible set; overflow the tail |
| Codecademy | 12 | Split into two tiers by scope |
| **Uxcel** | **13** | **Group under purpose labels** |

**Our baseline is the only one in the set with a flat, ungrouped, un-budgeted list.** That is
the finding — not that eleven is too many, but that eleven with no device is the outlier.

## Observation 2 — grouping earns its keep where the destinations differ in kind

Duolingo's eight are flat and work fine, and Babbel's three need no grouping at all. So grouping
is not universally required. What distinguishes Uxcel is that its destinations **differ in
kind** — learning surfaces and career surfaces are different jobs — whereas Duolingo's eight are
all facets of one activity.

That is the test to apply to our eleven: do they differ in kind? `Catalog`, `Ladders`,
`Practice`, and `Challenges` are learning; `Evidence`, `Credentials`, `Applications`, and
`Opportunities` are career-facing; `Inbox` and `Referral` are neither. That reads as **two or
three kinds**, which is the condition under which Uxcel's device applies.

Recorded as an observation about when the device fits, not as a recommendation. The
recommendation belongs in the synthesis, where all six platforms can be weighed together.

## Observation 3 — the rail is the motivational column, and it is ordered by permanence (Q5)

Three cards, top to bottom: **commercial offer**, then **streak**, then **league**. The order is
stable across both home variants captured.

Progress here is entirely in the rail — the centre column carries only a per-course `6%` bar
inside the resume card. So Uxcel splits Q5 the same way Duolingo does (daily signal always
visible, in a side column) but without Duolingo's separate statistics destination in the
captured set.

Note the slot our rail-owning platforms all put first: **an offer, not the learner's progress.**
Uxcel leads the rail with an upgrade or team-plan card; Duolingo leads with the stats strip.
One of the two treats the most persistent real-estate as commercial.

*(Guard check: region and count only. Mechanic choice and register are settled by F2 and the
named `PATTERNS.md` entries.)*

## Observation 4 — the resume card is the whole ranking device (Q3)

`Continue learning` is the first section, and its card is wider, taller, and the only element
with a filled button (`Resume course`). Recommendations below carry no buttons at all — they are
cards with bookmark controls. So ranking is achieved by **section order plus sole possession of
a filled button**.

Two structural details worth carrying: the card states `Current Lesson:` by name, so the learner
resumes into a known place rather than a course; and it states `7h left`, a **remaining-cost**
estimate rather than a percentage-complete claim. Both reduce the cost of deciding to continue.

Our baseline's primary CTA (`Start · one task, then you're done →`) does something similar in
copy — it bounds the commitment — but places it two-thirds down the page with six other links
competing.

*(Guard check: ranking **device**. Does not restate `PATTERNS.md`'s "Single recommended next
step", which concerns *whether* to have one, not how it is ranked.)*

## Observation 5 — block order runs from the learner outward (Q2)

Ordinal sequence down the centre column: **your course → recommended for you → bulletin board →
events → external resources**. Each step moves further from the learner's own work: what you are
doing, what you might do, what the community is doing, what is happening, where else to go.

That is a clean, defensible ordering principle and the sharpest available contrast to our
baseline, whose first two blocks are a product tagline and a paragraph explaining the product's
design philosophy — content that belongs to the company, placed where Uxcel puts the learner's
own course.

Ordinal only. No fold position is measurable from a 768 × 521 crop.

## Caveats carried

- **Reference-library observation.** No first-party use, no live behaviour, no timing.
- **Point-in-time**, accessed 2026-07-29.
- **Reference 02 is a zero-course home**, cited for grouping and block order only. Its
  zero-state composition is settled by `2026-07-28` F2 and is out of bounds here.
- **Rail persistence beyond `Home` is unobserved** — no non-`Home` destination was captured.
- **Sidebar behaviour at narrow width is unobserved.** Mobbin publishes no narrow web capture.
- **Audience transfer:** a paid product for working and aspiring designers in a Western market.
  The `GROW` cluster exists because its audience is job-seeking professionals. Our audience is
  younger and the career surfaces mean something different, so the *grouping device* transfers
  while the *specific groups* do not.
