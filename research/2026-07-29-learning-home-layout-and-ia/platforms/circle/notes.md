# Notes — Circle (analysis)

Source: Mobbin reference library, `web`, accessed 2026-07-29. **One screen.** Reference-library
observation, not first-party. **Non-education control**, captured under the plan's conditional
trigger because only one learning platform grouped its nav.

---

## Observation 1 — grouping is not an education convention (Q1)

This is the whole reason the platform is here. Uxcel groups its sidebar under `LEARN` and
`GROW`; the question that leaves open is whether grouping is something *learning products* do,
or something *products with many destinations* do.

Circle answers it. A community platform with no learning remit carries **fourteen sidebar
destinations under three labelled headings** (`Get Started`, `Product`, `Spaces`), plus two
ungrouped at the top, plus a five-item top nav.

So grouping is a **cardinality device, not a category convention.** It appears in two unrelated
product domains, in both cases where the destination count runs past roughly ten. That is what
makes Uxcel's `LEARN` / `GROW` transferable to us rather than an artefact of its being a design-
education product.

## Observation 2 — the group labels name the user's purpose, not the system's structure

`Get Started`, `Product`, `Spaces`. Uxcel's are `LEARN`, `GROW`. In both, the heading answers
*what am I trying to do*, not *what kind of object is this*.

Neither product groups by object type (no "Pages", "Tools", "Reports"). Two instances is thin,
but it is consistent, and it is the more useful reading for our eleven: a grouping of our nav
would be `learn / prove / find work`-shaped rather than `content / records / links`-shaped.

Two instances, one of them outside the domain. **Recorded as a weak signal, not a rule.**

## Observation 3 — a two-tier nav and a grouped sidebar are compatible (Q1)

Circle runs **both** devices at once: a five-item top nav for the workspace's main areas, and a
grouped fourteen-item sidebar for the spaces within. Codecademy runs two tiers without grouping;
Uxcel runs grouping without a second tier.

Worth recording because it means our four-way typology (`overflow`, `two-tier`, `grouped`,
`low-cardinality`) is **not mutually exclusive** — the arms can combine. The plan's disconfirming
clause anticipated a platform that fits none of the four; this is the opposite case, one that
fits two at once. The typology survives but should be described as a set of devices rather than
a set of categories.

*(This is exactly the sort of correction the disconfirming clause existed to catch, and it is
recorded here rather than smoothed over.)*

## Observation 4 — the rail leads with onboarding, not progress (Q5)

The right rail's first card is `Get started`, a three-item checklist with green checks; below it,
a `Members` list. There is no progress signal, no streak, and no counter anywhere on the screen.

Consistent with Circle not being a learning product — there is no progress to show. Recorded to
mark that **this platform contributes nothing to Q5**, so it is not counted toward Q5's
three-platform threshold.

## Caveats carried

- **One screen.** The weakest evidence base in the study. Circle may not be the sole source of
  any finding; it exists to corroborate Uxcel on Q1 grouping and does only that.
- **Not a learning product.** Audience-transfer caveat rides on every finding it supports.
- **Reference-library observation.** No first-party use, no live behaviour, no timing.
- **Point-in-time**, accessed 2026-07-29.
- **Contributes to Q1 only.** Not counted toward Q2, Q3, or Q5 thresholds.
- The screen shows a **trial banner** and a `Powered by Circle` mark, so it is a trial or
  lower-tier workspace. Not treated as bearing on structure.
