## Onboarding & activation

### Single recommended next step (goal → first meaningful action)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (Khan "UP NEXT FOR YOU!" on the course page and "Up next: Lesson 3" after each set — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/07-skill-summary-mastery.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 1, corroborated with Brilliant's smart-search recommendation-with-rationale — `research/2026-07-02-briliant/assets/smart-search-3.png`); `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` (the *onboarding* corollary — Khan's code-first `/join` routes a linked learner straight to their assigned content, whereas an un-routed learner is dumped into the full grade-organized catalogue and must self-locate — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/04-classcode-entry.png` vs. `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/06-catalogue-grade-organized.png`).
- **When it works:** Early drop-off starts *before* learning begins; collapsing "what do I do now?" into one obvious action lowers decision load and shortens time-to-first-practice. Strongest when the single step routes the learner *into practice*, not into another browse surface, and when the recommendation is chosen from the learner's actual mastery state. The onboarding study makes the "not another browse surface" clause concrete: routing straight to the assigned first task beats a catalogue the learner has to hunt through.
- **When it backfires:** "Recommendation theater" — if the suggested step is generic, or the system asks clarifying questions *after* recommending, the personalization claim rings hollow and trust drops. Whether the routing is truly adaptive vs. a fixed course pointer could not be confirmed from a single account, so do not oversell it as adaptivity.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-course-overview.png`, `research/2026-07-02-briliant/assets/smart-search-3.png`.

### Time-boxed, typed bite-sized lessons ("1 MIN")
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (lessons previewed as "VOCABULARY · 1 MIN · Let's go!" before entry — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/02-vocab-presentation-video-audio.png`). **Extended 2026-07-29 to the resume control** — `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F6) finds the same name-it-and-bound-it move on the *resume* control of a populated home in **3 of 7** platforms: [Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812) states `Current Lesson: The Anatomy of UI Components`, a `6%` bar, and `7h left`; [Coursera](https://mobbin.com/screens/18683325-d982-4756-b06e-4ed52c933b06) splits its card so the specific next item (`Welcome to module 1`, `Video (1 minute)`) sits beside a filled `Resume`; [Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/0fc493a3-6e4a-4571-ad2c-28e0cbce2908) leads its daily card with a category label, a title, and `⚡ 10 min` before the button, and puts duration before title on its past-topic cards too. Provenance for this data point is **reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured)**, cited by URL; it is also a **deliberately out-of-scope observation** of that study, which scopes copy out and routes F6 to a future content review.
- **When it works:** When the product's thesis is short daily practice ("ten minutes a day"), naming the lesson type and setting a one-minute expectation up front keeps perceived friction below the threshold that stops a habit forming, and makes in-session drop-off legible. **The resume case extends this from packaging a *new* lesson to re-entering an *unfinished* one**, and the mechanism is sharper there: resuming is a decision under uncertainty, and an unbounded "continue course" asks the learner to commit to an unknown quantity of time, where the safe answer is to defer. Preference uncertainty raises **choice deferral**, and deferral tracks the **absolute attractiveness** of the options rather than trade-off difficulty [ref: Dhar 1997 — `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` R7], so naming the item and bounding its cost raises absolute attractiveness and converts an open-ended commitment into a bounded decision about one small known thing.
- **When it backfires:** If the stated time is aspirational (a "1 MIN" lesson that actually runs several minutes), the promise erodes trust; the framing needs the real median time-to-complete to land near the label. Bite-sizing is packaging, not substance — it must wrap a real learning loop, not replace it. **Retracted at peer review (2026-07-29):** that stating **remaining cost** (`7h left`) outperforms **percentage complete** (`6%`) at low progress is **confounded and untested** — Uxcel's card carries `6%` *and* `7h left` on the same screen, so no capture isolates the two framings, and Mimo's `⚡ 10 min` is a duration estimate for a fresh item rather than a remaining cost. R7 covers deferral under preference uncertainty and does not cover this comparison. Do not design on it; it is the thing to A/B. The trust caveat above applies with full force to a resume estimate — instrument actual duration against the stated figure (the study fixes median actual within 25% of stated) and treat a systematically optimistic estimate as a defect, since it discredits every future estimate the product makes.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/flow.gif`, `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/02-vocab-presentation-video-audio.png`.

### Deferred, "try-first" registration (prove value before the wall)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — registration timing spans a spectrum across five apps: Duolingo defers the account entirely (language pick, intake, and placement all run in a guest session; the ask surfaces only at the `/learn` home, framed as *saving progress* — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-landing-value-framing.png`), Brilliant walls signup after the intake to "discover your learning plan" (loss-aversion on invested effort — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/07-signup-wall-discover-plan.png`), and CodeSignal is the extreme cold gate (a full name/email/password form before any value — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/codesignal/screenshots/01-signup-gate-create-account.png`), with Khan wall-first behind a role+age pre-step (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/03-signup-wall-social-email.png`). **Enriched 2026-07-16 with literature grounding** — `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature` (Feature 1) supplies the *theory and regulatory case* beneath the observed UI pattern: cross-industry mobile-onboarding benchmarks associate an upfront wall with a 25–40% initial session drop-off (directional consumer aggregate, **not** teacher-specific — `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/sources.md`); Perceived-Ease-of-Use predicts EdTech adoption among Indonesian teachers (Megawati et al. 2024, DOI `10.19109/intelektualita.v13i1.21095`; caveat: a *mandatory*-use TAM context measuring intention/satisfaction, so a directional analogy to a voluntary funnel, not direct funnel evidence); and — the strongest, most transferable anchor — a **structural access-exclusion** argument: under KepmenPANRB No. 16/2025, post-2024 honorary teachers (`Guru Honorer`) are locked out of Dapodik and never receive the `belajar.id` credential a login wall would demand. **First-hand wall-first anti-pattern captured 2026-07-17** — `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` (F1) adds a fresh live instance of the cold-gate failure: solve.education staging demands a date-of-birth and then a full name/email/password account **before any value at all** (landing → age gate → signup wall — `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/01-landing-hero.png`, `03-age-gate.png`, `04-signup-wall.png`), while a Duolingo first-win delta captured alongside it confirms the opposite order works — a guest reaches a first win in ~7–9 taps under ~2 min with the account ask deferred behind it (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/duolingo/flow-first-win-delta.gif`). **Unified and conditioned 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 1) re-synthesizes this study together with the teacher-literature and youth-onboarding studies and keeps value-before-signup at **High confidence** ([S1, S2, S3] — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md`), but its peer-review debate attaches two conditions that were absent here: a **verified-identity wall must still exist later** for anything credential-bearing, and deferral does **not** by itself repair a structurally broken login channel (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)`, findings 1 and 2). Both conditions carry their own entries immediately below.
- **When it works:** For low-context, email-scarce, first-time audiences, every field asked before value is delivered is a drop-off point. A guest session lets motivation build before commitment is required, and reframing the eventual signup as "save what you already made" converts loss-aversion into a *reason* to register rather than a barrier. Directly attacks the email-less-learner stall and the duplicate/abandoned-account problem that forced upfront signup creates. [ref: Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1991, loss aversion / endowment effect] The literature study adds a distinct, transferable rationale *beyond friction*: when the login wall depends on a credential a growing user segment **structurally cannot obtain** (post-2024 `Guru Honorer` locked out of `belajar.id`/Dapodik under KepmenPANRB 16/2025), deferring registration is an access-*inclusion* requirement, not merely a conversion optimization. Low-friction re-capture at the value moment (e.g. WhatsApp OTP / one-click login) is a *design inference* from the PEOU evidence, not itself an empirically tested mechanism.
- **When it backfires:** The named **anti-pattern is a cold account/assessment gate before value** — CodeSignal (and more mildly Khan) demand identity before showing anything, wasting even a strong placement mechanic behind a form a hesitant learner never crosses. The opposite failure is deferring so much that nothing is extracted to personalize the first task; the guest-to-registered **account-merge** is the load-bearing engineering risk (botch it and the duplicate-account pain returns in a new wrapper). Sits in direct tension with any "extract profile/level up front" model (e.g. a planned goal→level→profile→path): resolve *how much* personalization to pull before the first win vs. after. Do not over-borrow the literature's *numbers*: the 25–40% drop-off is a cross-industry consumer aggregate and the PEOU evidence comes from a *mandatory*-use civil-service context, so both justify *direction* for a voluntary teacher funnel, never a target magnitude — the teacher-specific effect is unmeasured (`research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Gaps & caveats`). **Population caveat (2026-07-17):** the guest-mode / deferred-registration evidence is overwhelmingly Western, high-connectivity *consumer* apps; for a mobile-first, potentially lower-digital-literacy job-readiness audience (solve.education youth) the *direction* of the lift should transfer, but the guest-mode mental model ("you can start without an account") may itself be unfamiliar and needs in-context testing (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/SYNTHESIS.md` F1). **Confidence correction (2026-07-20):** the unified study demotes the endowment/loss-aversion rationale from a *scientific mechanism* to a **proven design heuristic — loss aversion as a copywriting tactic** (Confidence: Medium), on the grounds that time invested at a wall is closer to sunk cost than to true psychological ownership [ref: Thaler 1980; Arkes & Blumer 1985 — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`]. Keep the framing; stop citing it as a mechanism. That study also re-lists the 25–40% drop-off figure under `## Refuted / weak claims` as a cross-industry consumer aggregate — direction only, never a target magnitude.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-landing-value-framing.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/codesignal/screenshots/01-signup-gate-create-account.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/07-signup-wall-discover-plan.png`. Caveat: desktop-web capture, single observed variant per platform; Duolingo's first *lesson* sits just beyond the captured guest session, and CodeSignal's assessment interior was left unseen by decision (not transacted).

### Verified-identity wall as the floor under deferred registration (credential-bearing products)
- **Kind:** literature-grounded design principle (litreview; no live UI observed)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 1, "Value-before-signup", Confidence: **High** [S1, S2, S3]) — raised by the Domain Expert and narrowed by the Evidence Auditor in `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` (finding 1: *defer the lightweight shadow profile early, but mandate identity verification later*). The counterweight rests on credentialing practice rather than on a captured screen: verifiable digital credentials and Learning & Employment Records require an identity the issuer can vouch for (1EdTech — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`).
- **When it works:** Deferring registration is right for the *funnel*, but for a product whose output is a credential an employer or institution is meant to trust, deferral has a floor. The guest / "shadow" profile can legitimately carry intake, placement, cohort code, and the first win; a **verified** identity must be attached before anything credential-bearing is issued. Naming the floor up front is what makes the try-first architecture safe to commit to: it separates the *funnel* question (when do we ask?) from the *assurance* question (what must be true before we certify?), so neither argument can be used to overrule the other. It also lets the verification moment be **designed as its own value exchange** ("verify to claim your certificate") — the same loss-aversion framing as the first wall, at a second, higher-stakes one where the thing being protected is genuinely worth more.
- **When it backfires:** This creates **two walls, not one** — if the verification step is not itself framed around claiming something earned, it merely relocates the drop-off to a later, more expensive point in the funnel. The principle is **standards/literature-derived from a desk study**: no platform in this library has been observed running a two-stage shadow → verified identity model end to end, so the sequencing is an untested design position, not an observed pattern. Verification also drags in the exact surface a guest funnel was designed to avoid (document/PII handling, consent, minor-data rules), so the floor must be decided *before* the guest funnel ships, not retrofitted. Finally, do not let "we need verified identity eventually" be used to argue the account wall back to the front — the whole content of this entry is that the two are separable. Cross-ref *Alternative low-friction identity channel* below: a phone-only identity may not be strong enough to underwrite a credential, so the two entries must be designed together.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 1 + `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 1), `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md`, `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`.

### Alternative low-friction identity channel (institutional SSO must never be the only door)
- **Kind:** literature-grounded design principle (litreview; no live UI observed)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 1, "Mitigating Structural Friction", Confidence: **High** [S2]) — the Skeptic challenged whether deferring registration resolves the underlying access barrier at all, and the Evidence Auditor shifted the recommendation from *defer the wall* to *decouple from the broken SSO and mandate an alternative low-friction login* (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 2). Builds on the structural-exclusion argument already recorded under *Deferred, "try-first" registration*, where a growing teacher segment is locked out of the government credential a login wall would demand (`research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature`).
- **When it works:** When a segment of the intended audience **structurally cannot obtain** the credential your login depends on — a government or employer SSO, a work email, a school-issued account — the fix is a second, genuinely independent door, not a better-timed wall. Deferring registration buys time; it does not create an account the user is *able* to make. Passwordless phone-number OTP (SMS / WhatsApp) is the low-literacy, low-bandwidth default because it asks for something the audience already has and demands no password recall. Generalizes to any product built on a third-party identity provider: "can every intended user actually obtain this credential?" is an access question to answer *before* the IdP is chosen, and the alternative channel must be first-class (same features, same entry points), not a degraded fallback buried under "having trouble?".
- **When it backfires:** An OTP channel carries its own costs and its own exclusions: per-message delivery cost and unreliable delivery on weak networks, number churn and recycled numbers (an account-recovery and impersonation risk), and shared or family handsets in exactly the low-income contexts this is meant to serve. **Evidence limit:** the structural lockout is documented for one national context ([S2], Indonesian teachers); that passwordless OTP *measurably* lifts completion for this audience is a **design inference, not a measured result** — the study logs no conversion evidence for the channel itself, so treat it as the hypothesis to test rather than a settled win. It also interacts with the floor above: a phone-only identity may be too weak to underwrite a credential, so plan the upgrade path instead of assuming OTP is the end state.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 1 + `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 2), `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md`.

### Endowment quality at the save-wall: a *created artifact* endows more than passive completion (design hypothesis)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (literature-grounded); **untested design hypothesis**
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature` (Feature 2) — a *desk-literature* study (no live UI), building on the endowment effect (Litovsky, Loewenstein, Horn & Olivola 2022, *PNAS* 119(34):e2202700119, DOI `10.1073/pnas.2202700119`) and SDT autonomy-supportive framing (Silva et al. 2025, DOI `10.28945/5549`). **Corroborated 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 2, "The Nature of the Endowment Object", Confidence: **Medium** [S1, S2, S3]) restates the audience split as a finding in its own right: generic learners are motivated to save XP, streaks, or a lesson score, whereas professionals require a tangible artifact tied to recognized credentials before ownership bites (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md`).
- **When it works:** The loss-aversion "save what you made" wall (see *Deferred, "try-first" registration* and *Three-stage UX sequencing* below) is only as strong as the endowment it protects. This study's hypothesis: a passive quiz / XP completion generates *weaker* psychological ownership than a tangible artifact the user **created** through real labor (here, a generated `Modul Ajar / RPP` lesson plan), so triggering the save-wall right after artifact creation — framed *informationally* ("save your work so it isn't lost & claim your certificate") rather than administratively ("create an account to continue") — should convert better. The loss > gain *direction* is literature-consistent (endowment theory + SDT informational framing).
- **When it backfires:** This is an **untested design hypothesis, not a demonstrated result** — no cited source establishes that a created-artifact endowment beats quiz/XP completion; validate with an A/B (gain-framed vs. loss-framed post-artifact modal) before relying on it. The loss-aversion magnitude ($\lambda \approx 2.2$) is **illustrative and actively contested** (2024 meta-analysis $\lambda \approx 1.955$, high heterogeneity; 2025 robustness challenge), so it indicates *direction only*, never a transferable conversion multiplier; any "≥18% lift" figure is an illustrative placeholder, not evidence-derived. Controlling / surveillance framing negates the SDT benefit. **Refines** the *Three-stage UX sequencing* Stage-3 example (which reframes the wall around saving "XP and personalized plan"): the hypothesis predicts a *created artifact* would endow more strongly than XP — a refinement to test, not a settled contradiction of that benchmark-observed pattern. **Demotion recorded 2026-07-20:** the unified study's peer review demotes the endowment effect from a *scientific mechanism* to a **proven design heuristic (loss aversion as a copywriting tactic)** and lowers its confidence, distinguishing true psychological ownership [ref: Thaler 1980] from time merely invested, which is sunk cost and a weaker behavioural driver [ref: Arkes & Blumer 1985] (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`); it independently re-lists the λ ≈ 2.2 multiplier under `## Refuted / weak claims`. That *raises* the bar for this hypothesis rather than lowering it: the artifact has to produce real ownership, not just absorb effort.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/SYNTHESIS.md` (Feature 2 + `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/sources.md`; `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 2 + `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 3).

### Single unambiguous CTA on the value-framing landing
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Duolingo's landing is near-empty: one benefit headline, one dominant primary CTA ("Get started"), one de-emphasized "I already have an account", nothing competing (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-landing-value-framing.png`). Contrast: Khan leads with a role chooser (Student / Family / Teacher) as the primary action, front-loading an identity decision before any value (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-landing-role-split.png`); Elsa proves value on the page itself with an embedded working test (see "Assessment-as-onboarding" below).
- **When it works:** A single high-contrast CTA with no competing elements removes ambiguity about what to do next, which is critical for low-context and low-tech-literacy users who will not hunt for the right control. Directly addresses the "primary CTA mistaken for an ad/banner" problem: when the button is the only salient action, it cannot read as a promo. [ref: Hick 1952, choice reaction time]
- **When it backfires:** When the first screen instead asks for a decision (role) or competes with marketing promos, the intended action loses salience and reads as marketing rather than a button to press. A single-CTA layout also assumes one clear primary audience; genuinely distinct entry paths (learner vs. teacher/admin) may need routing, but that routing should not be the *first* thing the value-seeker meets.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-landing-value-framing.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-landing-role-split.png`. Caveat: desktop-web, single observed variant (onboarding is heavily A/B-tested).

### Optional, positively-framed placement fork (novice and advanced from one screen)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Duolingo offers two side-by-side choices, "Start from scratch (take the easiest lesson)" and "Find my level (let Duo recommend where you should start)": placement is an opt-in fork, never a gate (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/08-choose-path-fork.png`). Brilliant goes further with autonomy support: after an advanced self-pick (Calculus) it *recommends* a foundation course but still lets the user jump straight to the hard content (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/06-start-point-recommended.png`). (Pairs with the recognition-based selector below: this entry is the *framing/optionality*, that one is the *mechanic*.)
- **When it works:** Framing placement as "let me help you find the right start" removes the social threat of a test, so a novice is not intimidated and an advanced user is not forced through basics; serving both paths on one screen lets a single flow cover the full ability range without branching the UI. Recommending foundations while permitting the jump nudges mastery without removing control. [ref: Ryan & Deci 2000, self-determination theory / autonomy support]
- **When it backfires:** A "recommendation" the user cannot override becomes a gate again and loses the autonomy benefit. If the fork's two options are not visibly different in stakes, a novice may still fear the "Find my level" branch as a test; the positive framing has to be explicit. Mis-placement (advanced user under-placed, or novice over-placed) needs a cheap in-task correction path, since a single self-selection is a coarse signal.
- **⚠️ Contradiction (flagged 2026-07-20):** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 3; `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 4) **discards the optionality** for a credential-bearing product: where the platform's output depends on a defensible baseline, the assessment is structurally required, so it argues to *mandate* the baseline while keeping this entry's constructive framing and its recognition-based mechanic. The two readings rest on different products rather than on contradictory observations — the optional fork is first-hand benchmark-observed on consumer learning apps (Duolingo, Brilliant), while the mandatory reading is a desk-literature position for a job-readiness platform that must be able to certify a starting level. **Unresolved:** whether a *mandatory* assessment can retain the autonomy benefit this entry rests on [ref: Ryan & Deci 2000], or whether "mandatory but positively framed" simply reintroduces the evaluation apprehension the fork was designed to remove (cross-ref *Pre-signup scored "aha" slice framed as proof, not a test*). Keep both readings; decide per product, not at library level.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/08-choose-path-fork.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/06-start-point-recommended.png`; `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 3 + `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 4).

### Level selection by recognition, not by label
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Brilliant's level selector shows an actual worked problem on each escalating card plus a first-person capability statement ("Arithmetic — I want to start from the basics", `5 × ½`, up to "Calculus — I understand derivatives and integrals"): the user recognizes the math they can do rather than self-rating a label (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/05-level-self-select-by-problem.png`). Duolingo runs a lighter version: a self-rated ladder rendered as signal-strength bars, "I'm new" (one bar) to "I can discuss most topics" (four bars), encoding level visually as well as verbally (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/04-proficiency-self-select.png`).
- **When it works:** Recognition is more inclusive than abstract self-assessment and lands for every user type: a low-literacy or non-native learner can identify a problem or a signal-bar level without parsing "beginner/intermediate/advanced" vocabulary, an advanced learner instantly spots their tier, and a novice is not shamed by jargon. Triple-encoding the choice (worked example + topic name + "I can…" statement) means at least one cue lands. [ref: Standing 1973, recognition-memory superiority]
- **When it backfires:** Recognition-memory research supports the *inclusivity/ease* claim; that recognition-based placement is *more accurate* than label self-rating is a **hypothesis, not a demonstrated result** — do not sell accuracy without the diagnostic-agreement study. Authoring a real worked problem per level is more content cost than a text radio list, and the exemplar problems must themselves be well-calibrated or they misplace users.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/05-level-self-select-by-problem.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/04-proficiency-self-select.png`.

### Assessment-as-onboarding, delivered before the wall
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Elsa embeds a genuinely working speaking test on its marketing page (a sentence to pronounce, an audio model to hear, a mic to record) reachable with no signup or download (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/03-web-speaking-assessment.png`). CodeSignal is the cautionary inverse: its signature AI skill-assessment placement is identical in spirit but sits entirely behind a mandatory account form, so a prospective learner never reaches it (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/codesignal/screenshots/01-signup-gate-create-account.png`).
- **When it works:** An assessment that is also a demo lets users feel competence and see the product's value in the first minute (a strong activation + acquisition hook) and produces the placement signal for free. Sequencing it *before* the wall (Elsa) vs. behind it (CodeSignal) is the difference between "try then commit" and "commit to try" — the former fits a hesitant, low-commitment audience far better. [ref: Roediger & Karpicke 2006, testing effect — supports only that a real assessment is genuine learning, *not* the before-the-wall conversion claim, which rests on the Elsa-vs-CodeSignal contrast]
- **When it backfires:** A real scoring engine can be a **High-effort, novel-ML workstream** (Elsa's pronunciation scoring) that must not be smuggled into an MVP; decouple the *sequencing* pattern (un-gated first task) from the *scoring* build. Watch the honesty of the demo: in this capture Elsa's mic fired on a silent clip and the AI score appeared only as a marketing mockup, so the scoring was observed *as claimed, not as a returned result* — do not overclaim a live score you have not seen. Speaking assessments also need permission priming (below) before they work on real devices. **Sequencing is necessary but not sufficient (2026-07-17):** placing a *scored* task before the wall does not make it a *win*. `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` (F2) shows that for a novice a *failable, unguided* scored first task can backfire (self-efficacy erosion, evaluation apprehension) — so the score must be framed as **proof, not a test** and heavily scaffolded (see *Pre-signup scored "aha" slice framed as proof, not a test* and the *winnable × credible × ownership-worthy trilemma* above). solve.education staging is the wall-first inverse: its scored role-play is withheld *entirely* behind the account wall, so no assessment-as-onboarding is reachable at all (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/04-signup-wall.png`).
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/03-web-speaking-assessment.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/codesignal/screenshots/01-signup-gate-create-account.png`. Caveat: Elsa's true onboarding is app-only (we captured the marketing site + web test + app mockups); CodeSignal's assessment interior was not entered.

### Code-first, chrome-free linked entry to assigned content
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Khan's `/join` page is a single-task screen ("Join your class on Khan Academy") with a segmented, fixed-length code input and nothing else: no nav, no catalogue. A valid code routes the learner to their assigned class; an invalid code is rejected inline (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/04-classcode-entry.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/classcode-flow.gif`). Without a code, the learner faces the full grade-organized catalogue and must self-locate (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/06-catalogue-grade-organized.png`).
- **When it works:** For learners who arrive via a facilitator, teacher, or program link, a code-first entry collapses the hardest navigation problem (finding the right course among many) into one deterministic step. Stripping the page of all other UI keeps a low-context user focused on the single action, and a segmented input makes the code format self-evident and cuts entry errors. This is the onboarding-time expression of "route into the task, not into a browse surface" (see *Single recommended next step*). [ref: Hick 1952, choice reduction; Sweller 1988, cognitive load] **Composition rule added 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 5, Confidence: **High** [S1]; `## Design implications` #6) resolves how this pattern coexists with deferred registration instead of fighting it: capture the program/cohort code into the **pre-registration guest ("shadow") profile** at entry, use it to route the learner to their assigned first win, and **lock it to the identity at the eventual wall**. Cohort attribution then survives a try-first funnel in which no account exists at entry time — which is what usually forces teams to put the account wall back at the front for reporting reasons.
- **When it backfires:** Only pays off when program/facilitator links are a genuine acquisition channel — for pure organic self-signup there is no code to enter, so it is a conditional build, not a universal one. A chrome-free page also strands a user who arrived *without* a code and needs the catalogue, so the entry point that lands them here matters. The 2026-07-20 guest-code-then-lock composition **enlarges** the guest→registered merge risk named in *Deferred, "try-first" registration*: the merge now carries cohort attribution as well as progress, so a botched merge loses programme reporting too, not only XP. Locking the code at the wall also needs a defined correction path for a learner who entered the wrong code before the win, and the code must be treated as attribution data with the same retention/consent care as the rest of the shadow profile.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/04-classcode-entry.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/06-catalogue-grade-organized.png`; `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 5 + `## Design implications` #6).

### Permission priming with a no-penalty fallback
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Duolingo primes then prompts: the mascot says "I'll remind you to practice so it becomes a habit" and *then* the browser notification prompt fires in context (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/07-notification-permission-primer.png`); its speaking tasks also degrade gracefully via a "can't speak now" escape hatch that marks the item done with no penalty and temporarily disables speaking (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/10-placement-speaking-mic-skip.png`). Elsa is the cautionary opposite: its web speaking test fires the mic on click with no rationale-first screen (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/04-mic-recording-active.png`).
- **When it works:** Users deny permissions they do not understand, and a cold system prompt (especially the OS-level mic prompt on mobile) is a common dead-end for low-tech-literacy learners who cannot recover a blocked-permission state on their own. Priming raises grant rates by supplying context immediately before the prompt, and a no-penalty fallback ensures a denied or missing permission never halts the flow. [ref: Felt et al. 2012, contextual/runtime permission requests]
- **When it backfires:** The named **anti-pattern is the un-primed hardware permission** — Elsa's cold mic-on-click (on a first-time device this is the raw OS prompt that strands users). Note the capture caveat: on this pre-granted browser profile no OS prompt actually appeared, so the *cold-prompt dead-end* is the well-established failure mode inferred for a first-time device, not something observed firing here. On-device permission handling (and the mic scoring engine behind it) is a real mobile-only risk surface the desktop-web capture could not exercise.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/07-notification-permission-primer.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/10-placement-speaking-mic-skip.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/04-mic-recording-active.png`.

### Character-guided, icon-first, low-text intake
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — all three character-led apps use a mascot as a friendly guide (Duolingo's Duo, Brilliant's Koji "I'll be your personal tutor", CodeSignal's Cosmo) and pose each question as a short prompt with pictorial choice cards (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/03-intake-why-learning.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/02-motivation-intake.png`). Brilliant extends personalization to the guide itself, letting the user choose the tutor's voice. **Corroborated 2026-07-16 by literature** — `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature` (Feature 3) *recommends* a lightweight, icon-first context selector that segments by the user's exact real-world role (`Jenjang: PAUD/SD/SMP/SMA/SMK`) and routes into short 5–15 min async modules, grounded in Indonesian infrastructure-gap research (Kurniawati 2025, DOI `10.32550/wdstp587`) — a literature *recommendation*, not an observed UI. **First-hand observed instance 2026-07-17** — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 8): Ruangguru greets users in Bahasa and segments by schooling level on the very first screen ("Ingin tahu produk untuk jenjang apa?" → PAUD / SD / SMP / SMA → *Pilih kelas*), a live example of the segment-by-exact-role recommendation for exactly this audience (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/ruangguru/screenshots/01-bahasa-onboarding-modal.png`).
- **When it works:** A guide character lowers cognitive load and adds warmth, turning a form into a conversation that reassures first-time and low-context users they are being led; icon-first, low-text choices cut the reading/typing burden that blocks low-literacy and non-native learners; personalizing the guide creates early ownership that motivates completion. The **icon-first** half is the cheap, high-leverage part (standard components); the animated guide character is a costlier add-on. [ref: Lester et al. 1997, persona effect; Sweller 1988, cognitive load] Independent infrastructure/adoption research for low-bandwidth Indonesian teacher contexts corroborates the low-text, icon-first, **segment-by-exact-role** approach and a short (5–15 min) async module length as an *access requirement*, not just a nicety (`research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature` Feature 3) — the segment cue also front-loads cultural/role relevance and lowers overhead.
- **When it backfires:** A guide character carries art/animation and (if voiced or "tutor"-framed) content/ML cost, and can feel patronizing to advanced users — make it skippable/quiet for them. Icon cards only work if the icons are unambiguous; a poorly chosen pictogram is *harder* than the word. Low-text intake still has to capture enough signal to route, so it trades richness for reach. **⚠️ Scope boundary flagged 2026-07-29:** the icon-first argument does **not** extend to persistent navigation. `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F7) finds 3 of 4 phone homes pairing each tab icon **with a text label**, and the one platform that drops labels (Duolingo, at six tabs) is the only one that had to; textual interfaces are unusable for first-time low-literacy users *and* glyph-only interfaces are error-prone for literate novices [ref: Medhi Thies et al. 2011, TOCHI — `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` R13, abstract only]. Icon-first belongs to **intake choice cards**, where a pictogram carries one concrete concept; a **navigation destination** is abstract and learned over time, so the label is the fallback the icon cannot be. See *The phone tab budget: three to five destinations, icon and label* (Navigation, home IA & layout). Neither reading is flipped; the surfaces differ.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/03-intake-why-learning.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/02-motivation-intake.png`.

### Bounded, reversible onboarding momentum scaffolding
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Duolingo brackets its questionnaire with a progress bar and a back arrow (finite and reversible), reinforces value with an outcome projection ("That's 50 words in your first week!" — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/06-habit-projection-50-words.png`), gives instant positive feedback on placement answers ("Nice!"), and deploys an exit-intent retention modal on mid-placement quit. Brilliant threads credibility/reassurance interstitials between questions ("You can make progress in both subjects later on"); both tease a locked feature with a concrete unlock condition ("complete 3 more lessons to start competing").
- **When it works:** A visible, *bounded* progress indicator reduces the anxiety of an open-ended form and *reversibility* lowers the stakes of each choice; outcome projections and instant positive feedback supply motivation to continue; teasing a gated feature with a clear condition converts overwhelm into a single next goal. Together they sustain a low-context user through a multi-step flow. The **bounded progress bar is the near-free win**; the trigger-driven pieces (exit-intent modal, projections) are add-ons to defer. [ref: Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, goal-gradient; Nunes & Drèze 2006, endowed progress]
- **When it backfires:** This is a grab-bag, not one atom — decompose it and adopt piece by piece rather than shipping "momentum" wholesale. An outcome projection ("50 words") that the product does not actually deliver erodes trust like any aspirational promise. Exit-intent modals shade into dark-pattern territory if they trap rather than reassure.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/06-habit-projection-50-words.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/flow.gif`.

### Deep localization of the onboarding chrome (not just content)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` — Elsa geo-redirects to a fully localized site (Indonesian, on an `id.` subdomain) where the value proposition, feature descriptions, and CTAs are all in the learner's language, not just the course content (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/01-landing-localized-value-framing-paywall.png`). **Corroborated 2026-07-17** — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 8): Ruangguru's onboarding *chrome* is Bahasa-first (the level-segmentation modal is fully in Bahasa — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/ruangguru/screenshots/01-bahasa-onboarding-modal.png`), and the study's cross-platform read is that for Indonesian teachers anything English-first meets friction, so Bahasa chrome is an adoption requirement, not a nicety.
- **When it works:** For learners studying a foreign language at low proficiency, native-language *chrome* (instructions, buttons, value framing, error messages) is the difference between comprehension and abandonment: a learner who cannot yet read the target language must still understand what to do. Auto-detection removes a manual language-picker step. Best treated as a **day-one i18n architecture decision**, since it is ruinous to retrofit. [ref: L2 reading cognitive-load / UI-translation studies] **Principle sharpened 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` (Theme 4, Confidence: **High** [S1]; `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 6) keeps the *behavioural* mandate (non-native UI imposes high extraneous cognitive load, so ship localized chrome) and explicitly **drops the technical geo-routing mandate**: automatic locale detection is one implementation of the principle, not the principle itself [ref: Plass et al. 1998, cognitive load in reading a foreign-language text with multimedia aids — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`].
- **When it backfires:** Localizing only the lesson content while leaving the interface in English recreates the exact comprehension wall these learners face — the half-measure looks localized but is not. Translation is an ongoing content pipeline (strings, errors, projections) that scales with every surface added; geo-redirect logic also has to be right, or it strands users in the wrong locale. Read with the 2026-07-20 sharpening, a geo-redirect is *not* required to satisfy this pattern — an explicit, persistent language switch serves the same behavioural requirement — so over-indexing on IP-based routing buys the strand-in-wrong-locale failure mode for no principled gain.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/elsa-speak/screenshots/01-landing-localized-value-framing-paywall.png`; `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Theme 4 + `## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 6).

### Three-stage UX sequencing architecture (minimal intake → first win → contextual save wall)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` (`REVIEW.md` §4, resolving `SYNTHESIS.md` Pre-win vs. Post-win extraction tension across Duolingo, Brilliant, Khan Academy, and CodeSignal).
- **When it works:** Resolves the structural tension between needing user data (goal, level, path) to personalize the first lesson versus the high drop-off of upfront registration (`Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler 1991`, loss aversion). Divided into three strict stages: (1) **Minimal Routing Intake (Guest Session, <45s):** Collect only Goal/Motivation + Level Selection (worked problem pick). Ask zero PII, avatar, or device permissions. (2) **First Interactive Win (Playable Task, 1–2m):** Route straight into a calibrated mini-lesson or assessment (`F5`) with progress bars (`H1`) and immediate positive feedback (`F9`). (3) **Contextual Account Wall (Post-Activation):** Reframe registration around saving earned value (`F1`): *"You mastered Level 1! Create an account to save your 50 XP and personalized plan."* Offer 1-tap social authentication (`CodeSignal H4` pattern) alongside email.
- **When it backfires:** If account merge from the guest session during Stage 3 fails or loses progress state, users lose their earned XP, destroying trust instantly. Sits in opposition to **cold account gates before value delivery** (e.g. CodeSignal upfront registration), which strand users before they experience product value. **Fresh first-hand counter-instance + stage sharpening (2026-07-17):** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` captures solve.education staging as a live wall-first flow (the exact anti-pattern this architecture avoids — `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/04-signup-wall.png`) and sharpens two stages when the win is a *scored* (failable) task rather than a Duolingo-style recognition win: **Stage 2** must be winnable + framed as *proof, not a test* + scaffolded (a novice failing a cold scored task backfires — see *Pre-signup scored "aha" slice…*), and **Stage 3**'s loss-aversion save-wall must be gated on a *genuine* win, since the IKEA effect vanishes on failed labour — ownership over a low/failed score can *depress* signup, so give a weak attempt a mastery-framed "keep practicing" fallback rather than "save your proof."
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/REVIEW.md`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-landing-value-framing.png`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/brilliant/screenshots/07-signup-wall-discover-plan.png`, `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/SYNTHESIS.md` (F5 + `## Peer Review`).

### Humanizing touches at the registration wall (signed personal commitment)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` (`SYNTHESIS.md` §F11, `REVIEW.md`; secondary practitioner study / public case studies).
- **When it works:** Placing a brief, warm, personal note (signed by a founder, teacher, or product team) right at the registration or transition wall humanizes the product and invokes social reciprocity (`Nass 1994`, Computers as Social Actors). A user feels they are interacting with human educators rather than a cold corporate form, reducing friction and anxiety at the exact moment commitment is requested.
- **When it backfires:** If the note feels overly automated or corporatized, or if it makes unfulfillable promises (e.g., "White-glove 1-on-1 support" that cannot scale), it erodes trust. Must remain authentic, scalable, and sincere. **Evidence quality flagged 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` places the headline case-study numbers in its `## Refuted / weak claims`: the reported ~65% transition uplift (and ~2× referral lift) comes from a high-price, high-touch B2B product's own published playbook, is **not peer-reviewed**, and does not transfer to a high-volume, low-context education audience — 1:1 human onboarding simply does not scale here. Only a **scalable** humanizing touch (a signed note, a named human voice at the wall) is the transferable hypothesis, and it is untested for this audience. The social-actor rationale beneath it still stands [ref: Nass, Steuer & Tauber 1994].
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/SYNTHESIS.md`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/REVIEW.md`; `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (Feature 11 + `## Refuted / weak claims`).

### Contextual just-in-time education (no front-loaded tutorial tours)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps` (`SYNTHESIS.md` §F12, `REVIEW.md`).
- **When it works:** Front-loaded tooltips and multi-screen walkthroughs ("Welcome! Click here, then here, then here") impose cognitive load before the user has context or intrinsic motivation (`Sweller 1988`). Removing front-loaded tutorials and instead explaining UI tools *only right when the user encounters the task requiring them* (just-in-time / progressive disclosure) preserves attention and keeps the user in the active learning loop (`Zeigarnik 1927`).
- **When it backfires:** If complex features are hidden with zero discoverability and no contextual hint, users may never find them. Avoid complex checklist engines or multi-step tour overlays in early MVPs; rely on self-evident affordances + contextual single-tool hints.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/SYNTHESIS.md`, `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/REVIEW.md`.

### Pre-signup scored "aha" slice framed as proof, not a test (no-fail, mastery-framed, scaffolding-gated)
- **Kind:** benchmark-motivated; **untested design hypothesis** (the solve.education scored win was never observed in capture; rests on an unfielded moderated test)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` (F2, the named aha) — Duolingo's first lesson item is a *recognition* win ("Which one of these is 'coffee'?", answered with a green check + "Nice job!"), engineered to be **won**, not to test (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/01-first-task-new-word-coffee.png`, `02-first-win-nice-job.png`, `flow-first-win-delta.gif`). The study transfers this to a solve.education *scored job-role-play* slice whose result is shown as **proof of what the learner just did, not a pass/fail test**, reachable as a guest — a slice the live build instead withholds entirely behind the account wall (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/04-signup-wall.png`, `SYNTHESIS.md` F2). Distinct from *Assessment-as-onboarding, delivered before the wall* (which is about *sequencing* an un-gated assessment ahead of the wall); this entry is about the *framing and winnability* the scored task itself must satisfy for a novice.
- **When it works:** When the aha is a scored slice of the *real* product (a job task the product certifies), the felt competence is exactly what the product promises to prove — more on-brand and differentiated than a generic recognition win. It holds only if the score is framed as **proof, not a test** (a mastery frame, not a performance frame) and first-task scaffolding (see *Bounded, reversible onboarding momentum scaffolding*) is heavy enough to make the win near-certain, so a true novice cannot fail at the cold-start moment.
- **When it backfires:** A *scored, failable, unguided* first task aimed at a novice is a documented failure mode, not a win: early failure erodes self-efficacy [`Bandura 1977`], a difficulty that helps an expert can hurt a novice [expertise / desirable-difficulty reversal — `Kalyuga 2007`; `Yan et al. 2023`], and scoring introduces evaluation apprehension a recognition tap avoids [`Jerrim 2023`]. The transfer from Duolingo's *un-losable recognition* win to a solve *failable scored* win is the study's load-bearing, **unobserved** leap; the scaffolding is a **hard precondition**, not an enhancement, and productive failure does not rescue a scored-first-task onboarding (it needs a safety norm during the struggle and instruction afterward, both absent before the signup decision) [`Kapur 2016`]. Watch an overjustification tension: an XP/score layer must reinforce, not crowd out, the intrinsic competence win [`Ryan & Deci 2000`]. Treat as a hypothesis to prove with a moderated first-run test. Cross-ref the trilemma entry below.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/02-first-win-nice-job.png`, `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/04-signup-wall.png`, `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/SYNTHESIS.md` (F2 + `## Peer Review`).

### The winnable × credible-as-proof × ownership-worthy trilemma (a pre-signup scored-aha design tension)
- **Kind:** **design tension / open design question** (untested); benchmark-motivated
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` (`SYNTHESIS.md` `## Gaps & caveats` — named as "the central open design risk", and running through F2, F5, and the named aha). Names the unresolved question beneath any pre-signup *scored* aha: can one slice be all three at once? **Re-affirmed and formally demoted 2026-07-20** — `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` carries the trilemma forward and its peer review rules it **Unsupported as a finding**, demoting it to a *primary research hypothesis requiring moderated usability testing* (`## Peer Review (2026-07-20)` finding 5, restated in `## Evidence gaps for primary research` and `## Gaps & caveats`). It adds the theoretical frame for *why* the three properties trade off — challenge–skill balance [ref: Csikszentmihalyi 1990] over intrinsic vs. extraneous cognitive load [ref: Sweller 1988] (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/references.md`) — without resolving it, so the entry's status is unchanged: still an open tension, now with a named validation method.
- **When it works (i.e., when the tension is resolvable):** The three properties can co-exist only under specific engineering: the slice is scaffolded so a true novice wins (**winnable**); the task is a genuine slice of the real job so the score reads as evidence worth trusting (**credible as proof**); and it leaves a concrete score/artifact worth keeping (**ownership-worthy**) so the loss-aversion save-wall (see *Endowment quality at the save-wall*, *Deferred, "try-first" registration*, *Three-stage UX sequencing*) has something real to protect. When all three hold, one slice serves the first win *and* the signup lever.
- **When it backfires:** Flow theory / challenge–skill balance suggests the three cannot all be maximized at once: make the slice trivially winnable and it stops being credible proof (and risks reading as the ad-like "fluff" the study's own interview learners already detect); make it credibly hard and a novice can fail it at the worst possible moment; deliver a win with no kept artifact and the save-wall has nothing to endow. This is an **unresolved trilemma, not a solved pattern** — the primary thing a prototype + moderated test must settle before Features 2/5 and the deferred-registration play can be trusted for this audience. Do not assume one scored slice can hold all three.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/SYNTHESIS.md` (`## Gaps & caveats` — the trilemma; F2, F5 + `## Peer Review`).

### Reposition the age / date-of-birth gate to the point of persistence (not the entry toll)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (first-hand anti-pattern)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` (F6) — solve.education staging makes a date-of-birth "Safety check" the **very first** action after "Get started free", before any value is shown (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/03-age-gate.png`). The copy itself is careful and honest (neutral framing; a clear 18+ / 13–17 / under-13 routing rule; a reassurance that DOB is not stored unless onboarding completes) — the problem is purely *position*. No benchmarked app opens with a bare PII form; the personal-data ask consistently comes later, at account creation.
- **When it works:** Keeping the child-safety age gate but firing it where PII and persistence are actually required (the "save your progress / create account" step) lifts a low-trust, PII-adjacent ask off the cold-entry path, so a first-time visitor reaches value before being asked for personal data. This is a **reposition, not a removal** — the safety requirement is legitimate; only its placement is at issue.
- **When it backfires:** Whether gating at persistence (before any stored PII or account) still satisfies the child-safety requirement is an **open legal/compliance question for sign-off (COPPA / GDPR-K / local child-data rules), not a fact a UX desk session can settle** — do not assert "protection stays intact" without legal review. Watch age-floor consistency across surfaces too (this study surfaced a live-copy **13+** vs internal-spec **15+** vs scope **15–36** divergence to reconcile). The unexercised routing screens (13–17, under-13) are a separate, unverified surface.
- **⚠️ Contradiction (flagged 2026-07-20) — placement and floor both unresolved:** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns` recommends the **opposite placement** — a **15+ age gate immediately after the landing screen, before onboarding begins** — justified by the legal minimum working age across Southeast Asia for a workforce / job-readiness product (`research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Design implications` #7 and `## Conclusion`). That is an entry-toll gate, precisely what this entry records as an anti-pattern *of position*. **The evidentiary status of the two positions is asymmetric and should be weighed, not averaged:** this entry's reposition rests on a first-hand captured flow (`research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment` F6, `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/03-age-gate.png`), whereas implication #7 appears only in the unified study's implications and conclusion — it carries **no `[S#]` citation, no confidence label, and no corresponding verified claim in** `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md` (whose own reusable-pattern list still reads *"Repositioned Compliance/Safety Gates (at persistence, not entry)"*). It is therefore a designer / compliance position carried by that study, not a verified finding of it. Separately, the **age floor remains unreconciled** across surfaces: live copy 13+, internal spec 15+, stated audience scope 15–36. Benchmarks do not settle it either — Khan runs an upfront date-of-birth gate behind its role chooser (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/02-age-gate-date-of-birth.png`), while the deferred-registration exemplars ask for nothing at entry. **Neither reading is flipped here.** Placement and floor are a legal/compliance × UX decision requiring human sign-off with legal input; untested middle grounds exist (e.g. a non-blocking eligibility statement at entry with the binding, data-collecting gate at persistence) and should be treated as hypotheses, not as the resolution.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/platforms/solve-education-staging/screenshots/03-age-gate.png`, `research/2026-07-17-youth-onboarding-aha-moment/SYNTHESIS.md` (F6); `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/SYNTHESIS.md` (`## Design implications` #7), `research/2026-07-20-unified-onboarding-synthesis-and-patterns/evidence.md`.

### The cardinality device (a navigation needs a sorting rule, not a shorter list)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F1) — every benchmarked platform applies one or more structural devices so that destination count stops being the operative variable. Learning platforms carry 3 to 13 destinations. Five devices are observed and they **combine rather than exclude**: grouping by **purpose** ([Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812) — 13 sidebar destinations under two small-caps headings, `LEARN` and `GROW`); two simultaneously visible **tiers by scope** ([Codecademy](https://mobbin.com/screens/df5c6018-fbf8-4473-88ba-4f7c02ba17ad) — 6 top-bar items *and* 6 sidebar items, things that exist above, things you have beside); a split by **type** ([Coursera](https://mobbin.com/screens/18683325-d982-4756-b06e-4ed52c933b06) — 4 learner tabs on a row beneath a utility row holding search, cart, language, account); a permanent-slot **budget with an overflow tail** ([Duolingo web](https://mobbin.com/screens/7baac688-9b35-4fc1-bbfd-f8bf8803a8f5) — 8 sidebar items ending in `MORE`); and a **shallow top level** that pushes everything into sub-levels ([Babbel web](https://mobbin.com/screens/5c06bda9-0458-437b-a68d-89ce396237e7) — three text links). The non-education control forced a correction to the study's own typology: [Circle](https://mobbin.com/screens/869cdce6-e3e5-4f7a-a447-9a09ac91e15a) carries 14 sidebar destinations **plus** a 5-item top nav, runs **grouping and two tiers at once**, and places two singletons (`Getting Started`, `Feed`) **ungrouped above** its three labelled groups — the same ungrouped-head-then-groups shape Uxcel uses.
- **When it works:** A device converts serial scanning of N labels into a two-step lookup (pick the cluster, then scan inside it), so cost tracks **cluster size** rather than list length — which makes 3 / 6 / 4 the relevant numbers, not the raw destination count [ref: Cowan 2001, working-memory capacity near four chunks — `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` R2]. The load-bearing consequence for design: **a high destination count is not itself the defect.** The two platforms in the set carrying the *most* destinations (Uxcel 13, Codecademy 12) both apply a device and both mark current location, so the reachable fix is a rule, not a deletion. The device also encodes an editorial judgement the learner can reuse: Uxcel's `LEARN` / `GROW` split tells them the product has two purposes before they read a single destination name. Three build constraints are observable rather than inferred: headings are **static labels with every destination visible** (`uxcel/flow.md` step 1); **no group in the whole set is smaller than three**; and singletons sit **ungrouped at the head** (Uxcel and Circle both). Grouping is a **cardinality device, not an education convention** — the non-education control shows it outside education entirely.
- **When it backfires:** **The five devices are not co-equal.** Larson & Czerwinski ran 512 items in three arrangements and found increased depth harmed search performance, with the *medium* breadth-and-depth structure beating the broadest-shallow one on reaction time and lostness [ref: R1], so Babbel's push-depth-down arm carries a cost the other four do not. Report R1's other arm honestly too: the broadest-shallow condition also lost to the medium one, and no arm of a 512-item 8³ / 16×32 / 32×16 experiment resembles an eleven-item nav, so it rules out depth without certifying breadth. **Collapsible groups are unobserved in this study** and would convert breadth back into depth, which is the arm R1 penalises. **No legibility or scanning measure was taken on any platform** — what the captures establish is that a device is *present*, not that a grouped nav is faster to use; treating the tally as a usability claim is the main misreading available here. The typology's own boundary is unstable in two ways: device presence is **not monotonic in cardinality** (3 → device, 4 → device, 5 → none, 6 → device), and Speak's `Course` / `Practice` segmented control is the same sub-level device that earns Babbel its entry, which on the typology's own definition would move the count from 7 of 9 to 8 of 9. The cardinality *breakpoint* was retracted at peer review; there is no observed threshold below which a device is unnecessary. An alternative explanation the study cannot rule out: nav size may track **commercial scope** rather than IA craft (Uxcel's 13 include `Salary Explorer` and `Job Board`; Codecademy's 12 include `For Business` and `Live Learning`), in which case the "device" is a consequence of heterogeneous business surfaces rather than a chosen solution. **Regime calibration:** "cost grows with list length" is a *linear* search claim that holds for scanning **unfamiliar** labels; for choice among **known** alternatives the relation is logarithmic [ref: Proctor & Schneider 2018, Hick's-law review — R3], so a flat list's penalty is largest at first encounter and shrinks with familiarity. Name the regime before invoking either.
- **Transfer status:** the **device** transfers; the **specific groups** do not — Uxcel's `GROW` cluster exists because its audience is job-seeking professionals. The closest source in the set to our own audience points the other way and should not be waved past: with 24 **emergent users**, a 5-page flat list was navigated *faster* than a 5-level hierarchy in the GUI condition [ref: Padhi et al. 2018, IndiaHCI — R12, **abstract only, never sole support**], which hardens the static-labels-all-visible constraint rather than the grouping. Every benchmarked label was measured **in English**; W3C, citing IBM globalization guidance, puts expansion at **200–300%** for source strings up to 10 characters [ref: R9], and nav labels are the shortest strings in a product, so any grouping must be re-checked in the shipping language. **Validation path:** an **open card sort (n≈15) run in Bahasa Indonesia** — the grouping rule must be the learners', not ours, and an English card sort validates labels that never ship — then a **first-click test** on the resulting variants, stratified by **facilitated versus self-directed arrival**, since a facilitator saying "tap Practice" values stable, permanently visible, nameable destinations differently from a self-directed learner.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F1, DI1, `## Nav cardinality comparison`, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/uxcel/references.md`, `.../codecademy/references.md`, `.../circle/references.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R1–R3, R9, R12).

### Marking the learner's current location in the navigation (and its second job)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured) — **visible half only; see the asymmetry below**
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F2, DI2) — **7 of 7 platforms, 9 of 9 surfaces** mark the active destination, in six treatments of one job: a filled pill ([Duolingo web](https://mobbin.com/screens/7baac688-9b35-4fc1-bbfd-f8bf8803a8f5), [Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812), [Circle](https://mobbin.com/screens/869cdce6-e3e5-4f7a-a447-9a09ac91e15a)); an underline ([Babbel web](https://mobbin.com/screens/5c06bda9-0458-437b-a68d-89ce396237e7), [Coursera](https://mobbin.com/screens/18683325-d982-4756-b06e-4ed52c933b06)); a filled row with a left edge bar ([Codecademy](https://mobbin.com/screens/df5c6018-fbf8-4473-88ba-4f7c02ba17ad)); a tinted icon plus darker label on the phone platforms ([Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/e466340b-80ce-4a91-b557-14d55afcf4c9), [Speak](https://mobbin.com/screens/a7b59b62-1a06-49b9-b386-5508bbfe97af)). It is the only structural convention in the study with **no exception anywhere in the set**.
- **When it works:** It answers the "where am I" half of orientation, and the cost of its absence lands hardest on the **returning** learner who arrived from a notification, a bookmark, or a facilitator's link rather than from a click they remember making. **Its second job is the reusable insight:** Codecademy marks the active state **only in the sidebar**, never in the top bar, on every captured screen — so the marking says where you are *and* which of the two visible tiers is the one you navigate within. That makes current-location marking a **required companion to the two-tier device**, not an optional polish on top of it. WCAG 2.2 **SC 2.4.8 *Location* is Level AAA**, and its sufficient techniques are exactly **G128** (indicate current location within navigation bars) and **ARIA26** (`aria-current`) [ref: W3C, WCAG 2.2 — R4]. Name the level honestly: this is not an AA obligation, so the case for shipping it rests on orientation for returning learners rather than on baseline conformance. It is the cheapest structural pattern the study produced — one attribute and one style rule. Ship the **visible** treatment first, since that is the half the benchmark supports and the binding constraint for a learner scanning a page, then `aria-current="page"` alongside it.
- **When it backfires:** **The evidence is asymmetric and the entry must not be read past it.** All seven benchmark readings are **visual** — a library still can show a filled pill or an underline and nothing more. Nothing in this study establishes that any benchmarked platform exposes `aria-current` or any programmatic equivalent, because a still cannot show it. So the visible half stands at 7 of 7; the **semantic half rests on R4 alone**, and citing the benchmark for it would be a fabrication. A treatment carrying the state in **colour only** fails colour-independence and has to clear 1.4.11 non-text contrast, so tint-alone (the phone platforms' treatment) needs a second cue. Verify with a comprehension check ("which of these are you on?") rather than an accessibility audit alone: an audit confirms the state is exposed, not that learners read it.
- **Transfer status:** device-level and audience-neutral — every platform in the set marks location and the mechanism does not depend on audience. The **least-hedged pattern this study produced**, and the one to spend the smallest budget on.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F2, DI2, `## Nav cardinality comparison`, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/codecademy/notes.md`, `.../babbel/notes.md`, `.../mimo/notes.md`, `.../speak/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R4).

### Learner-first block order (the company's content goes below the learner's own work)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F3, DI3) — on a **populated, returning-learner** home the first block belongs to the learner or to the material, in **6 of 7** platforms. [Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812) opens with `Continue learning` and a resume card, then `Recommended for you`, then [events and an external resources grid](https://mobbin.com/screens/484afd14-1406-48d8-9612-ff1ac2dba068). [Coursera](https://mobbin.com/screens/18683325-d982-4756-b06e-4ed52c933b06) opens with `Continue learning` and a weekly-goal card, then a career-goal strip, then [degree merchandising](https://mobbin.com/screens/558911ab-f326-4158-ab38-8e27a21ca639). [Duolingo web](https://mobbin.com/screens/7baac688-9b35-4fc1-bbfd-f8bf8803a8f5) opens with the unit banner and the lesson path: the material itself, immediately. [Babbel web](https://mobbin.com/screens/5c06bda9-0458-437b-a68d-89ce396237e7) opens with the learner's name above the tab switch and all content. [Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/e466340b-80ce-4a91-b557-14d55afcf4c9) opens with learner counter chips, a path selector, then the unit pill and path. [Speak](https://mobbin.com/screens/a7b59b62-1a06-49b9-b386-5508bbfe97af) opens with a level chip and a streak chip, then `UNIT 1` and the staggered path. **Two published positions sit inside the rule:** Duolingo states **where you are in the material**, Babbel greets **the person**. The study declines to prefer one; what matters is that something belonging to the learner or the material occupies the slot either way.
- **When it works:** A returning learner arrives with an intention already formed, so any content above their next action is a tax on that intention, paid every session. The measure that makes this more than taste: **57% of viewing time falls above the fold and 74% within the first two screenfuls**, with a sharp drop after [ref: NN/g, 120 participants, >130,000 eye fixations at 1920×1080 — R11, full text read], so the first two screenfuls are the scarce resource the rule allocates. Deferring company content costs nothing structurally — Uxcel carries **three further blocks** on its populated home at no cost precisely because they all sit below the thing the learner came for. The reusable ordering principle is Uxcel's: move **outward from the learner** at every step (what you are doing → what you might do → what is happening → where else to go). Coursera states the boundary more bluntly: there is a point in the page after which nothing belongs to the learner, and it comes right after the goal strip. Naming that boundary explicitly is the cheap part of adopting this pattern.
- **When it backfires:** **The counter-case is instructive, not sloppy.** [Codecademy](https://mobbin.com/screens/df5c6018-fbf8-4473-88ba-4f7c02ba17ad) inverts the rule — a full-width promotional countdown bar above the entire application, then a bootcamps carousel, and only then `Keep learning` with the learner's actual progress; all four of its captured screens carry at least one commercial slot. It has a documented commercial motive, and a subscription or bootcamp product with a real merchandising job will fight this rule for reasons that are not a mistake. Recorded as observed context, **not a model**: the honest question is which blocks earn the top of the page, not whether commerce is allowed anywhere on it. **Fold position is not measurable on any benchmarked platform** — Mobbin stills are fixed crops (web 768 × 521, iOS 299 × 678) carrying no viewport height — so this rule is **ordinal only**, and any "above the fold" claim about a benchmarked platform would be invented. A **specific** block order is also not readable off the benchmark: no two platforms in the set run the same sequence, so what transfers is the principle (the learner's own work first, company content after), never a particular ordering. **The rule is silent on a variable our audience has and theirs does not:** ordering blocks by attention never asks what each block costs to fetch and render, and every benchmarked platform optimises for capable devices and unmetered data — GSMA Intelligence prices an entry-level internet-enabled handset at roughly **16% of average monthly income** across low- and middle-income countries, rising to **48% for the poorest quintile** [ref: R19, abstract only, never sole support]. No lens in this workspace covers performance (`/extract-tokens` is colour, type, and spacing; `/a11y-audit` is WCAG), so byte cost will be missed by default unless it is asked for.
- **Transfer status:** the strongest in this section. The principle holds on 6 of 7 platforms, including the one closest to our audience on price and age (Duolingo), and it is the least audience-dependent structural finding the study produced. **Validation path:** a **five-second test** on the current order versus a reordered variant, asking "what can you do here?" and scoring the proportion naming the day's task, then an **A/B** on time-to-first-task-start and the share of sessions reaching a task within 30 seconds, with comprehension of the product's purpose as the guard metric.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F3, DI3, `## Gaps & caveats`, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/uxcel/references.md`, `.../coursera/references.md`, `.../codecademy/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R11, R19).

### Fill as the ranking device, spent once per screen (the feature-singleton precondition)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F4, DI4) — **every benchmarked platform (7 of 7) raises one action above the rest**, and on the four home screens where the full control set was enumerated none carries more than one filled control. [Codecademy](https://mobbin.com/screens/df5c6018-fbf8-4473-88ba-4f7c02ba17ad) is the clearest instance: a three-way control row (`View path`, `Start practice session 0/1 today`, `Resume →`) in which only `Resume` is colour-filled and the other two are plain text — **three adjacent actions and the ranking still reads**. [Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812) ranks by section order plus sole possession of a filled button (`Resume course` filled; the recommendation cards below carry no buttons at all). [Duolingo web](https://mobbin.com/screens/7baac688-9b35-4fc1-bbfd-f8bf8803a8f5) ranks with **no filled button anywhere**: the next node is larger and coloured against grey siblings and carries a `START` pointer. The same product changes device with width: on [iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/3ecf07e8-cea8-4414-b5d9-05cd21033429) Duolingo stops trusting position and lays an explicit full-width `START +25 XP` card over the path. **Position ranks in a wide multi-column layout; width and fill rank in a narrow one.**
- **When it works:** Fill is a single binary attribute resolved as a **feature singleton**: a target defined by one unique feature is detected in parallel with detection time flat in set size, whereas a target defined by a **conjunction** of features is searched serially [ref: Treisman & Gelade 1980, feature-integration theory — R5]. That names the precondition precisely, and it is not the obvious one: the requirement is **not that the screen carries one action**, but that exactly **one control owns the attribute**. This is why Codecademy can present three adjacent actions and still rank them, and it is what makes the device usable on a home that legitimately offers many things to do. Fill also degrades gracefully — it works in a single column, at any width, and costs nothing to build. **This entry extends *Single unambiguous CTA on the value-framing landing*** (Onboarding & activation), which is the *pre-login landing* case where one CTA with nothing competing is achievable and Hick's choice-count is the right law. On an authenticated home neither holds, so the transferable rule shifts from "one action" to "one *filled* control" and the mechanism shifts from choice count to the feature singleton.
- **When it backfires:** A second control taking the same fill converts a feature search into a **conjunction** search (fill *and* position *and* label), so a duplicated fill is a mechanism failure rather than an aesthetic one — the learner's first filled-button match is no longer guaranteed to be the intended action. Ranking with **no** filled control (Duolingo web's elevation-within-a-path) only works where a sequence supplies the position semantics; a page of unrelated cards has no path to be next in, so that variant does not generalise off a lesson path. **The scarcity claim is bounded to four enumerated home screens** (Codecademy reference 01, Uxcel reference 01's centre column, Duolingo web reference 01, Babbel's `Today`) and is *not* established over the study's 33 screens: two captured Babbel screens describe control sets whose fill was never recorded (`Learning plan`, a per-row `Start lesson` on five rows plus two rail buttons; `Explore`, a per-lesson `Start now`), so a universal negative is not available and should not be quoted as one. The **ranking-device** half is unaffected and holds on 7 of 7. **Two near-identical filled controls may be a symptom rather than a styling defect:** where a product ships two competing "today's task" concepts, demoting one to a text link treats content duplication as a fill problem and the second button gets rebuilt later. Run a **first-click test** designed to distinguish the two readings before choosing the fix, and instrument click distribution across the competing controls first so the baseline rate is known.
- **Transfer status:** device-level. The mechanism (a single distinguishing visual feature) does not depend on audience, and scarcity of that feature matters **more** for novice and low-literacy users, not less, since it is the cue that works before any label is read.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F4, DI4, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/codecademy/references.md`, `.../uxcel/references.md`, `.../duolingo/references.md`, `.../babbel/flow.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R5).

### Progress split by permanence (a daily glance on the home, the detailed record in a destination)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured) — **a documented range of arrangements, not a rule with a denominator**
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F5, DI5) — five distinct arrangements across seven platforms. [Duolingo web](https://mobbin.com/screens/7baac688-9b35-4fc1-bbfd-f8bf8803a8f5) runs three tiers: always-visible rail counters (streak, gems), always-visible rail cards (league, daily quests), and a separate [Statistics destination](https://mobbin.com/screens/6ff49359-fff4-4ea2-97f2-50097b0873ac) holding total XP, a weekly chart, and achievements. [Uxcel](https://mobbin.com/screens/54c0aa01-9380-4e13-bc41-01e46dba6812) puts the daily signal in a persistent rail ordered offer → streak → league. [Babbel iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/cec0c04f-7eaa-4e20-aa0e-c23642cb54c6) puts an activity tracker in the content flow and the record in a [profile sheet](https://mobbin.com/screens/65435f22-30a8-440c-9868-8fb64d41c7e9). [Speak](https://mobbin.com/screens/9d2d3577-07a1-45db-acf1-42dc269d0216) puts courses and a reverse-chronological activity log on `Profile`. [Codecademy](https://mobbin.com/screens/df5c6018-fbf8-4473-88ba-4f7c02ba17ad) dissents outright: progress is not a place at all but an attribute of whichever card owns the work (`1%` inside the resume card, `4 / 9 concepts practiced` inside a course row). **Clean fits — a small home signal plus a separate detail destination — are 3 of 7** (Duolingo, Babbel, Speak).
- **When it works:** A progress display answers two different questions at two different frequencies: *did I show up today* (every session, one glance) and *how am I doing overall* (occasionally, with attention). Collapsing both into the primary column means the frequent question gets answered by a block sized for the infrequent one, and the learner pays that cost on every visit. Keeping one persistent daily signal is supported: effort rises as a visible goal nears completion, and the effect tracks **perceived** progress rather than real progress [ref: Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006 — R6]. That also supplies the sharper test of whether a given display earns its space — **a signal with no gradient cannot produce the effect the space is being spent for.** Two devices in the set are worth isolating: **attach the meter to the thing it measures** ([Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/e466340b-80ce-4a91-b557-14d55afcf4c9) hangs a `58%` ring directly on the current unit's name pill, measuring one named thing rather than summarising the learner in the abstract), and **run two goal horizons in two registers** ([Coursera](https://mobbin.com/screens/18683325-d982-4756-b06e-4ed52c933b06) renders a **behavioural** weekly goal as seven weekday circles beside a **career-outcome** goal stated as prose with an `Edit goal` affordance; on [My Learning](https://mobbin.com/screens/ff884482-7343-4d72-b38d-74436b6dc450) both move into a right rail). For a *returning*-learner home the behavioural horizon is the more actionable of the two and the one most often missing entirely.
- **When it backfires:** **The separation is a range, not a rule.** Only 3 of 7 fit cleanly: Uxcel shows the home signal with no detail destination in the captured set, Mimo moves detail to `Practice` (a **peer tab**, with its `Profile` unobserved), Coursera's detail destination is unobserved, and Codecademy dissents. Codecademy's dissent is coherent and worth weighing before treating the split as settled — progress-as-card-attribute keeps the signal beside the work it describes. **The operative variable may not be region at all.** Learning-analytics dashboard research puts the weight on a display's **reference frame** — whether it compares the learner to peers, to their own past, or to an absolute standard — in which case relocating a block is half a change. The load-bearing source (Jivet, Scheffel, Drachsler & Specht 2017) was **located but not readable**, so this is recorded as an **open question, not a citation**; R17 (Vreugd et al. 2025, abstract only) indexes the construct and reports social-comparison frames as stressful for low-performing students. Resolve it before this pattern reaches a PRD. **Endowed progress has a hard boundary here:** artificial advancement raises persistence (34% redemption with two pre-granted stamps against 19% from zero) [ref: Nunes & Drèze 2006 — R15, abstract only], but the library's *Mastery signal tied to accuracy, not completion* (Mastery & progress signaling) forbids inflating a **proficiency** estimate, so any endowment belongs on a **behavioural** counter and never on a readiness or mastery figure. Finally, **no retrieved source tests the frequency-separation layout claim itself** — R6 supports the motivational half only, so the layout half stands on the captures alone. Cross-ref *Progress bar / goal-gradient as the low-risk default motivation mechanic* (Motivation & retention), which this **refines rather than contradicts**: that entry argues a visible near-term bar is the safest mechanic for a low-connectivity, collectivist audience; this one says *which* progress display earns home space (the daily one) and which does not (the detailed record).
- **Transfer status:** the region-and-count observation transfers. The two-horizon arrangement is **n=1** in the set and is a model to test, not a convention; the reference-frame question is unresolved and is the thing most likely to change the recommendation. **Validation path:** a **comprehension test** on a full versus reduced variant, asking learners to state their standing and what to do next, with a stated margin (the study fixes 10 percentage points of stated-standing accuracy) plus instrumentation treating any progress element interacted with in under 10% of sessions as a deletion candidate.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F5, DI5, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/duolingo/references.md`, `.../babbel/references.md`, `.../speak/references.md`, `.../mimo/references.md`, `.../coursera/references.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R6, R15, R16, R17).

### The phone tab budget: three to five destinations, icon *and* label
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured); **hypothesis for validation on Android mobile web** — every input is native iOS
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F7, DI6) — four iOS phone homes, all using a fixed bottom tab bar. [Babbel iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/6a031570-7a5a-4007-a0f0-4ccf5dc47e1b): three tabs, icon above text label (`Home`, `Review`, `Explore`). [Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/e466340b-80ce-4a91-b557-14d55afcf4c9): five, labelled. [Speak](https://mobbin.com/screens/a7b59b62-1a06-49b9-b386-5508bbfe97af): five, labelled. [Duolingo iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/02544e61-aefd-4d89-815e-ffcc83d83539): six, **icon only, no text labels**, terminating in a `…` overflow. **Three of four label their tabs; the one that does not is also the only one exceeding five and the only one needing an overflow.** Which destinations earn a tab, across the three platforms whose identities are assertable: a learning surface (3 of 3), a review or practice surface (3 of 3), a profile in 2 of 3 (Babbel has none). Mimo ships a `Leaderboard`, against any assumption that community surfaces do not earn a tab. Babbel's three iOS destinations are the **identical three** as its web nav, in the same order; Duolingo's *counts* differ (8 web, 6 iOS) but its iOS **set** cannot be compared, because the icon-only bar makes tab identities beyond the active one unreadable — the destination-*set* claim was **retracted at peer review**.
- **When it works:** A bottom bar's width is fixed by the device, so each added destination takes touch-target and label space from the others. Read it as a **budget**: past roughly five, either the labels go or a tail goes behind an overflow. **The half that matters most for our audience is the redundant coding, not the count.** Textual interfaces are unusable by first-time low-literacy users and error-prone for literate-but-novice users [ref: Medhi Thies et al. 2011, TOCHI — ethnography of 90 low-literacy subjects across four countries plus controlled studies with 70+; R13, abstract read in full, never sole support], and an unlabelled glyph leaves a novice nothing to fall back on. **Icon *plus* label is what 3 of 4 platforms ship, and it is what neither an icon-only bar nor a text-only row provides.** When a tail must go somewhere, the observed pattern is a **combo**: keep the majority permanently visible and put only the tail behind an overflow — Duolingo keeps seven of its eight web destinations visible behind `MORE`, and `duolingo/notes.md` records that the overflow is **not a hamburger replacing the nav**. That distinction is measured, not stylistic: hidden navigation costs more than 20% of discoverability against visible or combo, raises perceived task difficulty by 21% against visible, and is used in 27% of desktop cases against 48% visible and 50% combo [ref: NN/g, n=179, 6 sites — R10, full text read]. **A hamburger is a materially worse pattern than the one this evidence supports.**
- **When it backfires:** **The whole entry is a two-step transfer** — native iOS evidence for an Android **mobile-web** target — and no responsive *web* home at narrow width was observed anywhere in the study. Material Design's "three to five top-level destinations", with tap targets sitting too close together past five, converges with the four-app count [ref: R8], but treat that convergence carefully: it is a **normative specification, not a study**, the cited page is the **archived Material Design 1** spec scoped to **native Android apps**, Duolingo's own six-tab bar is a published counter-instance to it, and convention and sample plausibly share one ancestry, so the corroboration is **convergent rather than independent**. In an Android browser a fixed bottom bar additionally competes with collapsing URL chrome for scarce vertical height, which nothing here tested. **The five-slot budget is a pixel budget measured on English monosyllables.** Expansion runs **200–300%** for source strings up to 10 characters [ref: W3C citing IBM globalization guidance — R9], and nav labels sit in exactly that band, so a five-slot bar at 360px holding `Kesempatan` / `Tantangan` / `Kredensial` is a different problem from `Home` / `Review` / `Explore`; measure the localized label set at 360px before fixing any count. The label-space mechanism itself is **inferred from one platform** (Duolingo's trade of labels for icons at six), not measured, and this study holds no evidence on label length in any language but English. Also state explicitly what happens to the destinations that lose a tab — the choice between combo and sub-level decides whether this pattern helps or harms, and defaulting to hiding the whole nav is the failure mode R10 prices.
- **⚠️ Tension with *Character-guided, icon-first, low-text intake* (flagged 2026-07-29).** That entry argues icon-first, low-text choices cut the reading and typing burden for low-literacy learners. Read carelessly it licenses an **icon-only** navigation, which is the opposite of what this pattern and R13 support. Reconciliation, and neither reading is flipped: **icon-first belongs to intake choice cards**, where a pictogram carries one concrete concept and the screen has a single job; **persistent navigation destinations are abstract and learned over time**, so the label is the fallback the icon cannot be. `Ladders` or `Evidence` as an unlabelled glyph is the failure case both entries should be read as forbidding. Decide per surface, not at library level.
- **Transfer status:** the weakest in this section by construction. **Validation path:** a **tree test** on the shortlisted destinations (success criterion ≥80% task success) before any visual design begins, then a **moderated session on a real Android device at 360px**, since every input here is iOS. Note the study's own honest admission that a cheaper route existed and was not taken: `.claude/references/mobbin-sourcing.md` triggers **C2** and **C5** authorise a Chrome capture of a benchmarked platform's responsive web home at 360px, which is the cheapest way to raise this entry's confidence.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F7, DI6, `## Mobile home reference`, `## Gaps & caveats`, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/babbel/references.md`, `.../mimo/references.md`, `.../speak/references.md`, `.../duolingo/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/references.md` (R8, R9, R10, R13).

### Nothing stacks beneath the primary object on a phone home (move it, sub-level it, or drop it)
- **Kind:** reference-library observed (Mobbin; not first-party captured); **hypothesis for validation on Android mobile web** — every input is native iOS
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia` (F8, F9, DI6) — **4 of 4** phone homes dispose of the desktop's secondary blocks rather than stacking them beneath the primary object. [Duolingo iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/673d73bb-bb22-4481-8f14-7cf966c9e7bd) scrolls to reveal more lesson path and nothing else, and **drops** its league and quests from the home entirely. [Speak](https://mobbin.com/screens/a7b59b62-1a06-49b9-b386-5508bbfe97af) fits five named steps into roughly one screen by **staggering** cards horizontally rather than stacking them in a column, then puts courses and history on `Profile`. [Mimo](https://mobbin.com/screens/e466340b-80ce-4a91-b557-14d55afcf4c9) splits the home across two tabs **by rhythm**: `Learn` is the long arc through the curriculum and carries nothing else, while [`Practice`](https://mobbin.com/screens/3bd10950-73fa-4189-97f1-4c35e3400111) is the four-block stack (daily review, past topics, progress tiles, playgrounds). [Babbel iOS](https://mobbin.com/screens/cec0c04f-7eaa-4e20-aa0e-c23642cb54c6) pushes the rest down a sub-level (`Learning plan` plus a profile sheet). **Babbel is the recorded counter-instance to any stricter reading**: it carries an `Activity tracker` bar above the lesson card and a horizontal further-lessons row below it, so it *is* a stack. The claim that survives on all four is the **disposal** claim, not "one dominant object".
- **When it works:** A phone column holds roughly one meaningful object per screenful, so stacking N blocks costs N screenfuls of scrolling and buries whichever is Nth; tabs convert that vertical cost into a horizontal choice paid once. Mimo's split names the reusable division principle: **the arc and today's work are different surfaces with different visit frequencies, not two sections of one page** — a learner opening the app to do today's task and one reviewing their trajectory are in different modes, and one surface serving both serves neither well. Speak runs the same split under different names (`Home` for the unit path, `Profile` for courses and log). **The structural precondition is decided on desktop, not on the phone.** Across the four web platforms the side rail splits two ways: rail as **persistent shell** (Duolingo, Uxcel — present on every home view) and rail as **per-view content** ([Babbel](https://mobbin.com/screens/906d0d44-a4e4-4d54-800b-bba8a4dfe715), present only on `Learning plan`; Coursera, absent on `Home` and present on `My Learning`). A rail the layout does not depend on is cheaper to collapse at narrow width than one it does, which makes shell-versus-per-view a decision to take when the desktop layout is designed. This claim is recorded independently in `babbel/notes.md` Observation 4 and `coursera/notes.md` Observation 5, and it is the part of this entry **not** resting on two cases.
- **When it backfires:** **The tempting rule here is a hypothesis, and promoting it would be the main error available.** The two web-and-iOS pairs in the set handle the rail's loss in **opposite** ways — Duolingo deletes its league and quests, Babbel promotes its activity tracker **into the primary column** — and the *sought* versus *must-be-seen* explanation for that split (a competitive signal the learner seeks out costs little when dropped; a commitment device the learner cannot see stops being one) was derived from exactly those two opposite cases. Any two opposite observations admit a post-hoc partition, `babbel/notes.md` Observation 3 states the disciplined version (*two options, not a rule*), and no literature was found for the distinction. Treat it as the question to test, phrased as "does this stop functioning if unseen" rather than "does this fit". The structural shell-versus-per-view classification is stronger but its **consequence is inferred**: no responsive web home at narrow width was observed in this study, so the collapse was never seen happening. "One meaningful object per phone screenful" is likewise an unsourced structural reading of iOS captures. And splitting a home across tabs **relocates a discoverability problem rather than removing one** — whatever moves to `Profile` or `Practice` now sits behind a tab the learner must know to press, which is where the current-location and label-legibility entries above stop being optional.
- **Transfer status:** same two-step transfer as the tab-budget entry (iOS → web, iOS → Android), plus an untested assumption that a tab idiom survives a browser context at all. **Validation path:** classify each current signal *sought* or *must-be-seen*, prototype both treatments, and run a retention comparison against a stated threshold — the study fixes migration as justified only where deleting the signal costs more than about **5 percentage points** of 7-day return rate or weekly-goal completion, and deletion otherwise. Cheaper interim proxy: instrument the desktop home and treat any element interacted with in under 10% of sessions as a deletion candidate.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/SYNTHESIS.md` (F8, F9, DI6, `## Mobile home reference`, `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-29-learning-home-layout-and-ia/platforms/babbel/notes.md`, `.../coursera/notes.md`, `.../mimo/references.md`, `.../speak/references.md`, `.../duolingo/references.md`.

## AI tooling & generative UX

### Form-first generation on the user's own material ("no blank box")
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (tool grid first-hand; the "form replaces prompting" mechanic externally cited)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 1) — MagicSchool AI is a grid of discrete, job-named tools (*Lesson Plan, Rubric Generator, Multiple Choice Quiz, IEP Generator, Text Rewriter…*), each a short structured form (grade / subject / topic) that returns a complete first draft, deliberately removing the blank chat box (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/screenshots/03-teacher-tools-showcase.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/screenshots/05-ai-for-teachers.png`; the "pick a tool, fill a short form, no blank box to stare at" mechanic is externally cited in `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/notes.md`). Khanmigo reaches the same low-effort outcome via a chat tuned for "good results with minimal prompting" (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/screenshots/02-for-teachers-tools.png`).
- **When it works:** For time-poor, low-confidence users who lack the exact skill a blank chat box demands (prompt-writing), a structured form carries the cognitive load and guarantees a good first output, producing the fast "first win" that drives adoption and self-efficacy; job-named tools also do recognition work (a user recognizes "Lesson Plan," not "a large language model"). Strongest as the *first* rung, before any prompting is taught (see *Fluency ladder* below). Same "structure beats blank page" principle as *Scaffolded input (fill-the-blank, not blank page)* in the learning loop, applied to a generative tool rather than a practice item. [ref: Bandura self-efficacy / enactive mastery; Reijnen et al. 2023 — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/references.md`]
- **When it backfires:** A shield teaches the *least* transferable skill — a user who only ever fills forms never learns to prompt a generic tool, so form-first must be a first rung, not the whole ladder. The "7–10 hours saved / usable artifact in minutes" efficacy figures are vendor marketing plus login-gated reviewer reports, not independently measured — treat as claims to test, not established efficacy. The generative backend (and a Bahasa/locale quality+safety eval harness) is the real build cost; the form itself is trivial.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/screenshots/03-teacher-tools-showcase.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/screenshots/05-ai-for-teachers.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/notes.md`.

### Bounded, observed sandbox with supervisor tool-picking ("Rooms")
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed — **second-hand** (login-gated; mechanics externally cited, not observed by us)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 5) — MagicSchool "Rooms" / MagicStudent: a teacher creates a Room, hand-picks which of 50+ student tools are available for a given goal and maturity level, and every student interaction is logged and visible to teacher + admin via a *Student Room Insights* dashboard, framed as "teacher-led activities, safe settings for students." Khanmigo similarly gives teachers an oversight view of student tutoring (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/notes.md`).
- **When it works:** When a fluent supervisor (teacher, parent, admin) wants to let a less-experienced or minor user *use* a powerful tool without it becoming unsupervised or unsafe, a bounded surface (supervisor picks the allowed tools) plus full interaction logging gives control, visibility, and a defensible answer to stakeholders — which is what makes the supervisor willing to allow the tool into the setting at all. [ref: Scherer, Siddiq & Tondeur 2019 adoption / facilitating conditions; trust as adoption prerequisite, Scientific Reports 2025 — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/references.md`]
- **When it backfires:** Every mechanic here is **second-hand** (login-gated; sourced from external reviews, not observed) — confirm against a logged-in session or official docs before a spec depends on the specifics. It is also the heaviest build in the set: minors' PII + consent, a per-interaction logging pipeline, a dashboard, and a child-safe model — effectively a second product, best deferred to a later phase, not an MVP.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/notes.md`.

## Curriculum & sequencing

### Fluency ladder: shield → minimize → teach the skill
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (cross-platform stances first-hand on public surfaces; in-app depth externally cited)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 2) — three stances on the *same* skill (prompting) read as rungs of one ladder: **shield** (MagicSchool removes prompting; a form produces the draft — see *Form-first generation* above), **minimize** (Khanmigo keeps a chat tuned so users "get good results quickly with minimal prompting" — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/screenshots/02-for-teachers-tools.png`), and **teach** (Google AI Essentials makes prompting an explicit skill: a whole "Discover the Art of Prompting" course plus named "prompt patterns" — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/google-ai-essentials/screenshots/01-specialization-5-courses.png`).
- **When it works:** A shield gets the fastest first win but teaches the least transferable skill; teaching the skill builds durable capability but demands more confidence up front. Sequencing them resolves the tension: begin where success is guaranteed (shielded), then as confidence accrues expose and teach the underlying skill so it generalizes beyond any single tool. Generalizes beyond AI — any product teaching a hard skill can ladder from "we do it for you" → "we make it easy" → "you learn to do it." [ref: van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen 2010 (scaffolding = contingency + fading + transfer); Lazonder & Harmsen 2016 (guidance d = 0.50) — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/references.md`]
- **When it backfires:** If the ladder never fades the scaffold, the user stays dependent (the shield's transfer weakness). The "teach" rung often leans on generic third-party content you must actually author/localize. Validate the ladder against a *transfer task* (perform the skill in a generic tool with no scaffold) plus retention, not just first-session success.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/screenshots/02-for-teachers-tools.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/google-ai-essentials/screenshots/01-specialization-5-courses.png`.

### Sequence responsible-use / ethics after the first value win (ambient trust throughout)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (curriculum placement externally cited; trust surface first-hand)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 4) — responsible use shows up two ways: **ambient** (MagicSchool's wall of compliance badges + "we don't use your data to train AI" + an *AI-Resistant Assignments* integrity tool — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/magicschool-ai/screenshots/06-trust-and-safety.png`) and **as curriculum placed late**: Elements of AI puts "Implications" (bias, privacy, future of work) as the final chapter (6 of 6) and Google AI Essentials puts "Use AI Responsibly" as course 4 of 5, both after the productivity win (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/elements-of-ai/notes.md`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/google-ai-essentials/notes.md`).
- **When it works:** Front-loading ethics onto a fearful novice is abstract and demotivating; once a user has personally seen the tool be useful (and, ideally, seen it be confidently wrong), "verify everything" and "watch for bias" become lived lessons rather than slogans. Ambient trust/privacy cues meanwhile lower the adoption barrier for a risk-averse audience from the first screen. [ref: Knowles andragogy (adults are relevancy-oriented and problem-centred); trust drives continued teacher AI use, Scientific Reports 2025 — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/references.md`]
- **When it backfires:** "After the first win" is not "delayed to the finale": Elements defers ethics to the very end because it is a *conceptual* course, but if the user starts *using* AI early (e.g. from module 2), unsafe use can happen before a late ethics chapter — place responsible use after the first win but not at the finale. Ambient trust cues are only credible if the data-handling behind them is genuinely engineered; badges over un-engineered infra is a trust anti-pattern. The andragogy support is a framework, not an RCT on ethics-*sequencing* — A/B the module order (ethics-first vs. ethics-after-first-win).

### Zero-setup learn-by-doing practice player (active recall + immediate feedback)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (split-screen brief + in-browser code editor, Run Code / Submit — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/04-exercise-player-split-screen.png`); `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (fill-the-blank word tiles + match-the-pairs — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/03-complete-the-sentence-exercise.png`, `06-match-the-pairs-exercise.png`); `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (7-problem Algebra sets — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/flow.gif`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 2, incl. - **When it works:** The learner produces the answer (active recall) and is graded in seconds — *read/see → attempt → be judged → continue*. Removing environment/setup friction eliminates the highest-drop-off step, and the pattern generalises across domains (code, language, math). Active production also creates stronger *evidence of skill* than passive content consumption.
- **When it backfires:** A real execution/answer surface can carry heavy build cost (e.g. sandboxed execution of untrusted user code — the biggest hidden cost in DataCamp's player) and a per-item content pipeline that scales linearly with the catalogue. The mechanic is cheap to imitate; the content and infrastructure behind it are not.

### Scaffolded input (fill-the-blank, not blank page)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (code pre-seeded with blanks `____` — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/04-exercise-player-split-screen.png`); `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (numbered word tiles with keyboard shortcuts — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/03-complete-the-sentence-exercise.png`).
- **When it works:** Providing structure (blanks to fill, tiles to place) keeps difficulty in the "productive struggle" zone and out of "blank-page paralysis," letting a beginner act immediately. Numbered tiles / keyboard shortcuts further cut input friction for short lessons.

### Concept → exercise → reflect micro-unit loop (text-first, low-bandwidth, multilingual)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (course shape first-hand on landing; interiors externally cited)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 7) — Elements of AI pairs short plain-language readings with inline exercises and later peer-reviewed short essays across 6 chapters, framed to "demystify AI" and leave learners "empowered, not threatened … no complicated math or programming required … at your own pace" (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/elements-of-ai/screenshots/01-positioning-demystify.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/elements-of-ai/notes.md`); it reaches 2M+ learners across 170 countries in many languages. Google AI Essentials mirrors the shape: five short self-paced courses (~10h total, each 1–2h), 17 languages.
- **When it works:** Short concept→do loops keep cognitive load low and give constant small wins, suiting time-poor, low-confidence adults far better than long lectures; the explicit "demystify / not threatened" framing addresses learner anxiety directly; a **text-and-exercise** format (vs. heavy video) is the most tolerant of a low-bandwidth, mobile reality (e.g. many Indonesian teachers). Complements *Time-boxed, typed bite-sized lessons* (packaging short units) and *Zero-setup learn-by-doing practice player* (the exercise half of the loop). [ref: Sweller cognitive-load / worked examples 2010; Roediger & Karpicke 2006 (immediate exercise = retrieval practice) — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/references.md`]
- **When it backfires:** A single concept→exercise loop is *massed*, not spaced — do not claim a spacing / distributed-practice benefit unless the design adds deliberate spaced revisits of earlier units across days. The low-bandwidth claim must be *instrumented* on real devices/connections, not assumed. Multilingual delivery is an ongoing localization pipeline (strings, exercises, feedback), not a one-time translation.

### Grab-and-go, run-tomorrow lessons + structured "dilemma discussion" format
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (collection page first-hand; lesson interiors account-gated, externally cited)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 6) — Common Sense's AI Literacy Lessons for Grades 6–12: nine self-contained ~15–20-min lessons (*What Is AI? · How Is AI Trained? · Understanding AI Bias · … · Artificial Intelligence: Is It Plagiarism?*), several using a packaged "Dilemma Discussion" format, sitting inside a ~150-lesson, 100%-free curriculum with a per-grade scope & sequence (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/common-sense-ai-literacy/screenshots/01-ai-literacy-lessons-collection.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/common-sense-ai-literacy/notes.md`).
- **When it works:** A time-poor facilitator (here, a teacher) will not build a lesson from scratch; a short, self-contained, standards-friendly lesson is the exact altitude they can adopt with no extra prep. A packaged structured-debate ("dilemma discussion") format turns the hardest, most values-laden topic (e.g. "is this cheating?") into a facilitatable activity, lowering the fear of running it. Lesson *interiors* (slides/handouts) were account-gated and not observed, so the lesson count/timings/formats are first-hand but the pedagogical quality inside each is not. Western (US, English-first) source content needs a localization check before reuse in another classroom context.

## Feedback & recovery

### Feedback that teaches, not just scores (instructional feedback panel)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (wrong answer returns the correct sentence, replayable audio, and a plain-language rule — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/04-incorrect-answer-feedback.png`; right and wrong taught in the *same* panel shape — `05-correct-answer-feedback.png`); `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (wrong answer withholds the solution and offers a step-by-step worked path plus embedded video — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/03-incorrect-feedback.png`); `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (submit prints the code's actual output + a forward-looking message — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/06-submit-success-xp-reward.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 4, "highest-confidence pattern in the benchmark").
- **When it works:** Making the feedback panel a teaching surface (correct form + why + how to fix) turns the moment of being graded into the moment of learning, and removes the "punished for guessing" feeling that makes learners cautious. Teaching right and wrong answers with the *same* panel shape reinforces the rule either way.
- **When it backfires:** Person-level praise copy ("Nice work! You got it") is one of the *least* effective feedback types (Hattie & Timperley 2007, cited in the Khan study); positive feedback should point at the process or the mastery gained, not the person. Rich instructional feedback is also authored, item-attached content, so it carries an ongoing authoring cost per item.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/04-incorrect-answer-feedback.png`, `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/04-graduated-hint-and-video.png`, `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/06-submit-success-xp-reward.png`.

### Productive-struggle remediation (withhold the answer, give graduated hints)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (a miss yields "Not quite yet… give it another try" with "Show a step" revealed one step at a time + a related teaching video, never the answer — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/04-graduated-hint-and-video.png`); `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (tiered Take Hint → Show Answer — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/05-hint-scaffolding-xp-cost.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 3; Brilliant's Koji companion is positioned in-lesson but its full tutor behaviour was not observable — `research/2026-07-02-briliant/assets/ai-companion-1.png`). **Enriched 2026-07-17 by** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 3) — Khanmigo's Socratic, answer-withholding *tutor*: "challenges you to think critically and solve problems without giving you direct answers," shown responding "Let's solve it together" and guiding step by step rather than answering (`research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/screenshots/03-for-learners-socratic.png`, `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/khanmigo/screenshots/04-for-parents-tutor.png`; behaviour externally cited, login-gated). This extends the remediation pattern from a *practice-item hint stack* to a *conversational tutor* mode, and its "proper use by design" framing directly counters the "AI as a cheating machine" fear (the tool becomes a thinking coach, not an answer-vending machine).
- **When it works:** Being stuck with no path forward is the number-one churn moment in self-paced learning. Withholding the answer preserves the retrieval attempt (the part that builds memory) while graduated hints keep cognitive load manageable and keep the learner *in the task* instead of tab-switching to Google. The active ingredient is the **guidance, not the withholding**: guided discovery outperforms direct instruction (Alfieri et al. 2011, d = 0.50), but *unguided* discovery underperforms it (d = -0.38), so an answer-withholding mode must *actively scaffold* (leading questions, hints, step checks), never merely refuse the answer.
- **When it backfires:** Help titration is the hard part — learners can use hints as an answer-shortcut and clear tasks without learning (watch over-reliance as the failure mode). **A withholding mode that only refuses, without active scaffolding, can harm learning** (unguided discovery, above) — the refusal alone is not the win. Authoring graduated hints + a mapped video per item is a content pipeline from zero that scales linearly and never ends; a multi-turn tutor that must enforce "scaffold, not refuse" is additionally an open eval problem with per-turn inference cost and latency on poor connections.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/03-incorrect-feedback.png`, `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/05-hint-scaffolding-xp-cost.png`.

### Priced help (hints cost effort-currency)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (Take Hint −30 XP lowers the exercise reward 100 → 70; Show Answer −70 XP forfeits the rest; each hint asks "Was this helpful? Yes/No" — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/05-hint-scaffolding-xp-cost.png`).
- **When it works:** Charging the reward currency for help protects the "desirable difficulty" that makes learning stick — help is available but not free, so learners attempt first. The Yes/No prompt doubles as a hint-quality signal.
- **When it backfires:** If the price reads as punitive rather than a nudge, it can suppress help-seeking exactly when a learner needs it; test the copy/affordance so the trade-off is understood before the click. Pricing help only makes sense once an effort-currency (XP) already exists and is credible.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/05-hint-scaffolding-xp-cost.png`.

### Front-loaded real spoken/modelled input before practice
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (new vocabulary introduced as native-speaker video with replayable audio and a save-word bookmark, before the practice step — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/02-vocab-presentation-video-audio.png`).
- **When it works:** For skills where modality matters (language listening/pronunciation), modelling the target with real audio/video from the first step trains perception that text alone cannot, and gives the learner something concrete to imitate before they produce.

Effectiveness of a "speed control" or replay is only worth claiming to the extent it is actually observed (Busuu's was seen at 1× only).

## Mastery & progress signaling

### Mastery signal tied to accuracy, not completion (the progress bar *is* the proficiency estimate)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (per-skill mastery levels Not started → Familiar → Proficient → Mastered shown as a colour grid + mastery points, *gated on accuracy*: a two-mistake modal caps the attempt at "can no longer reach Proficient," so 5/7 earns Familiar, not Proficient — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/06-mastery-gate-modal.png`, `07-skill-summary-mastery.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 5, "mastery signals that mean something").
- **When it works:** Collapsing "what you've learned" and "your progress bar" into one accuracy-driven object makes the learner optimise the right thing — the reward *is* the proficiency state — and gives an honest self-assessment surface. The accuracy gate is the cheap mechanism that makes the expensive mastery signal trustworthy and stops grinding to a hollow badge.
- **When it backfires:** Done without a calibrated item bank / estimator, it is "a completion bar in a proficiency costume." Platform mastery is *near-transfer* (doing the platform's items), not demonstrated far transfer, so it should not be sold as proven real-world ability without delayed/transfer validation.
- **⚠️ Contradiction (flagged 2026-07-13):** Khan subordinates the visible progress signal to accuracy, whereas DataCamp (XP) and Busuu (Stars/Score) award their headline progress on *completion/activity*, which the meta-synthesis notes are "less clearly tied to mastery." Same surface ("your progress"), opposite guarantee — resolve per product: is the collected number meant to certify mastery, or just fuel habit? Do not silently treat XP-style points as a mastery signal.

## Motivation & retention

### Layered motivation stack (points + streak + daily challenges + time-boxed league)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (Stars/Score per lesson, daily streak flame, "Today's challenges" progress bars, weekly Blue League — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/10-todays-challenges.png`); `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (XP, Daily Streak, weekly Bit League — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/08-leaderboard-bit-league.png`); `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (energy points + streak, kept mostly separate from the learning-tied mastery scale — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-course-overview.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 6, "return loops with guardrails"); `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 8 — Ruangguru's *Starchamps*: points for correct/fast answers, badges, competition, reported ~30% engagement lift; the points/badges vocabulary is the engagement grammar Indonesian learners already recognize — `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/ruangguru/screenshots/01-bahasa-onboarding-modal.png`); `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F5–F7 decompose the same stack — Duolingo's XP HUD, flame streak, and time-boxed Daily/Monthly Quests with progress bar + countdown + progressive "unlock soon" reveal — `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/03-quests-and-badges.png`, `02-gamification-dashboard.png`).
- **When it works:** Learning fails on *consistency, not comprehension*; the stack attacks habit at three altitudes — frequent tangible progress (points), don't-break-the-chain (streak + daily goals via goal-gradient/loss-aversion), and a resetting social deadline (league) — answering "why open the app today?" A **culturally legible** points/badges vocabulary (Ruangguru-style) also lowers the learning curve for the motivation layer itself where a foreign metaphor would not land. **Framing lever (2026-07-17):** frame points/XP as **feedback** ("you earned 20 XP") rather than a **scoreboard** ("you're #14") — feedback plausibly complements competence (recruiting Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999's verbal-feedback effect, though only *by inference* since XP is a symbolic/tangible reward, not verbal praise), whereas a scoreboard tips the same currency into competition (see *Time-boxed peer league*, which is risky for collectivist audiences).
- **When it backfires:** Points/streaks are activity-tied and can reward presence over mastery ("hollow engagement"); research shows time-on-task can even move *opposite* to learning. Always pair retention metrics (D1/D7/D30) with a learning-gain/retrieval guardrail, and correlate points earned with actual learning so the currency rewards learning, not gaming. **Named anti-pattern (2026-07-17): rewarding *speed*** — Ruangguru's Starchamps awards points for *fast* answers, which optimizes haste over consolidation; reward *return / consistency*, not speed. **Forgiving-streak + offline-first requirement (2026-07-17):** for a connectivity-constrained, mobile-first audience (Indonesia: 356M mobile connections ≈ 125% of population, documented digital divide [UNICEF; DataReportal]) a *hard daily* streak punishes rather than motivates — a day missed for lack of data resets progress and breeds guilt. Use streaks **forgivingly** (streak freezes, weekly rather than daily targets), and treat **offline-first** (cached progress, no streak-punishment for no-data days, sync-when-connected) as an explicit design constraint on *every* mechanic in the stack, not just streaks. (Streak *effect* evidence in the 2026-07-17 study is vendor-sourced — Duolingo's blog — and flagged as industry claim, not proof.) The ~30% engagement-lift figure is a single non-peer-reviewed vendor-adjacent business-blog claim tied to Ruangguru's own AI+gamification bundle, and gamification meta-analysis (2023) finds points/badges lift engagement but barely move competency and shift focus to the reward — so lift is not learning.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/08-lesson-complete-stars-score.png`, `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/10-todays-challenges.png`, `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/08-leaderboard-bit-league.png`.

### Time-boxed peer league / leaderboard (resetting social competition)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (weekly Blue League, ranked table of ~20+ learners, own row highlighted — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/11-blue-league-leaderboard.png`); `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (weekly Bit League, "Gain 250XP to enter", "3 days left" — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/08-leaderboard-bit-league.png`); `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F8 — Duolingo **locks** the leaderboard for new users: "Unlock Leaderboards! Complete 3 more lessons to start competing," so competition is **earned/opt-in**, not imposed, and the locked state doubles as a tease — `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/02-gamification-dashboard.png`).
- **When it works:** A ranked, *time-boxed* league adds social proof and a deadline that manufactures urgency, then resets — so it motivates without permanently demoralising, and needs no permanent-loss punishment. **Earned/opt-in gating (2026-07-17):** delaying competition behind a few early successes (Duolingo's "complete 3 more lessons") lets a user build competence before the competitive layer appears and converts the locked state into a single next goal — the same pre-completion pull the credential uses. This is the reusable safe form of leaderboards: *earn it, make it optional, or reframe it to a shared goal.*
- **When it backfires:** A **demotivation tail** for bottom-ranked learners is the named harmed segment across the studies — segment by rank and consider opt-out / cohort-banding. It also needs critical mass to populate credible cohorts (matchmaking + weekly rollover jobs), so it is a "gate on scale," not a day-one build. Third-party names on the leaderboard are a PII-redaction surface. **Cultural caution (2026-07-17):** decomposed by element, competitive stacks are *small and sometimes negative* (Points+Badges+Leaderboards g≈0.32; some Levels+Badges+Leaderboards stacks reportedly negative [Zeng et al. 2024]), and gamification moves *competence* least [ETR&D 2024]. For a **low-individualism** (Hofstede IDV=14), high-power-distance, harmony-oriented, mobile-first audience like Indonesian teachers, public head-to-head individual ranking risks demotivating the majority who are not near the top — it is the **lowest-priority** simple mechanic for that audience. Prefer reframing to **cohort/school shared goals** (prosocial; see *Prosocial / collective framing*). The status-credential *paradox* reconciles this with the certificate lever: the same culture that discourages public individual competition still rewards status **credentials** — so lead with the certificate and keep individual leaderboards optional/reframed, don't drop status motivation altogether.
- **⚠️ Contradiction / contextual flag (2026-07-17):** The prior consumer language-learning studies (Busuu, DataCamp — Western-leaning audiences) present the resetting time-boxed league as a broadly **net-positive** urgency mechanic whose only real downside is the bottom-rank demotivation tail. The 2026-07-17 study, for a **collectivist Indonesian teacher** audience, ranks the *individual* leaderboard the **lowest-priority / riskiest** simple tool and recommends it be earned/opt-in or reframed to cohort/school goals rather than shipped as an imposed individual ranking. Same mechanic, audience-dependent verdict — resolve per audience: individual competition is a *default-on* urgency lever for individualist consumer learners but a *default-off, reframe-first* lever for a low-individualism professional audience. (The cultural claim rests on Hofstede IDV=14 applied by inference, not a measured Indonesian-teacher leaderboard study.)
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/11-blue-league-leaderboard.png`, `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/08-leaderboard-bit-league.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/02-gamification-dashboard.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F8 + `## Peer Review`).

### Post-task reward / celebration moment
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (lesson-complete screen: Stars +10, Score 80%, +3 words, confetti — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/08-lesson-complete-stars-score.png`); `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (end-of-set summary: new level, "🔥 You improved your skills! 🔥", energy points, "Up next" — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/07-skill-summary-mastery.png`); `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` (green ✓, +XP, live counter — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/06-submit-success-xp-reward.png`).
- **When it works:** Placing the reward beat immediately after real effort, tied to an *earned* result, plus an "Up next" nudge, uses goal-gradient momentum to pull the learner into the next rep while motivation is still high.
- **When it backfires:** Expected, activity-contingent tangible rewards (points) can *crowd out* intrinsic interest (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, cited in the Khan study); anchor the celebration to the *competence signal* (the mastery level) rather than the point total, or the point number becomes the headline. Two-scale mismatch is a real risk: Khan's set advanced mastery but left the weekly "Level up" meter at 0/1, which a learner reads as "I did the work but the game says 0."
- **⚠️ Contradiction (flagged 2026-07-13):** Busuu and DataCamp present the points/Stars celebration as an unqualified positive, while the Khan study — via cited motivation research — treats the same tangible-reward-after-task mechanic as a crowd-out *risk* to mitigate. Keep both readings: celebrate, but anchor to competence and guard against the reward becoming the goal.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/08-lesson-complete-stars-score.png`, `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/07-skill-summary-mastery.png`.

### Institution-recognized credentials as an activation/completion driver (and the vanity-certificate trap)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (literature-grounded)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature` (Feature 3) — a *desk-literature* study (no live UI): verifiable PD certificates (`Sertifikasi` / `Jam Pelatihan`) drive teacher adoption (Utomo & Lestari 2025; Setyaningrum & Susanto 2024, DOI `10.53008/kalbisocio.v11i2.4359`, single Bekasi district), against the credential-valuation framework Perdirjen GTK No. 7607/2023 (`RHK` / `e-Kinerja`). **Corroborated 2026-07-17 by** `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers` (Feature 8) — free recognized certification recurs across the benchmark as the native completion lever: PMM (the govt app every Indonesian teacher knows) already trains via ~5-hour self-paced modules with recognized completion certificates (context-only, external — site gated: `research/2026-07-14-ai-literacy-upskilling-indonesian-teachers/platforms/platform-merdeka-mengajar/notes.md`), and free-certificate-as-motivation also appears in MagicSchool (free Level 1–3 courses + "Pioneer" identity), Elements of AI, and Google's shareable career certificate. **Extended 2026-07-17** by `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F1) with the *global* benchmark UI and the underlying mechanism: Coursera frames the certificate as an external signal you display **before doing any work** ("Shareable certificate → Add to your LinkedIn profile" — `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/coursera/screenshots/02-shareable-certificate.png`) and Google for Education says it outright ("Certification looks good on you, and your resume" — `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/google-teacher-center/screenshots/01-certification-hero.png`). It supplies the mechanism (completion-contingent rewards raise *quantity*/completion but do **not** raise, and can modestly *undermine*, intrinsic interest [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999; Cerasoli et al. 2014]) and the culture anchor (Indonesia's 2007-08 teacher certification raised status/pay **without** improving teaching [Kusumawardhani 2017] — evidence of a credential-*signalling*, not competence, culture).
- **When it works:** For learners inside a structured professional / civil-service framework (here, Indonesian teachers), tying module completion to a credential that carries **official, institutionally-recognized value** (PD credit counting toward `Angka Kredit` / `e-Kinerja`) is a strong *extrinsic* completion driver — the credential, not the learning UI, is the goal. The reliably shippable version is **tier-1**: an immediately exportable artifact the user files into their *own* existing workflow (a `Modul Ajar / Bukti Karya` a teacher enters in their own `RHK`), which delivers real recognized value entirely under product control while also solving a daily job-to-be-done. The 2026-07-17 study adds the reusable **role-assignment** rule that resolves the cert-vs-badge question: make the certificate the *terminal* extrinsic credential (the reason to **finish**) and never the sole reward — pair it with lower-stakes **in-loop participation recognition** (badges/points/quests; see *Badge as in-loop participation recognition*) that carries the between-session engagement a terminal credential cannot. The extrinsic pull is highest for the low-formal-signal worker, the profile LMIC credential-*visibility* evidence shows benefits most [Athey & Palikot 2024, unrefereed preprint].
- **When it backfires:** The **vanity-certificate trap** — a standalone, unaccredited in-app PDF certificate carries *zero* official valuation (Perdirjen GTK 7607/2023), so a credential that only *looks* official drives no adoption and can erode trust. The stronger "tier-2" version — formal co-certification (`MGMP/KKG 32 JP`) carrying `e-Kinerja BKN` recognition — is an **open governance hypothesis, not a shippable feature**: recognized credit flows through the user's own institutional approval (a `Kepala Sekolah`-approved `RHK` entry) and depends on locally-autonomous bodies outside product control, so realizing it is a multi-year, district-by-district policy/partnership workstream (`research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/SYNTHESIS.md` `## Gaps & caveats`). Evidence skews to single districts (Bekasi / Bali), so the weight of credential motivation vs. infrastructure barriers may shift in remote / `3T` contexts. Generalize as: *credential recognition works only when the credential is genuinely recognized by the institution the user answers to — build the tier you actually control.* **Cross-ref:** the *Layered motivation stack* and *Post-task reward* entries caution that extrinsic tangible rewards can crowd out intrinsic interest (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999); a real professional credential differs from in-app points in that it is the user's genuine external career goal, but the crowd-out caution still applies to how heavily the credential is dangled over the learning itself. The 2026-07-17 study sharpens this into the *mechanism*: because the completion-contingent certificate drives completion but not intrinsic *quality*, if the design goal is durable intrinsic engagement (not just finishing) the certificate is the wrong lever to lean on — route intrinsic work through competence/participation feedback instead. Its *local transfer* to Indonesian teachers rests on only two directly-Indonesian anchors (PMM-enjoyment; the 2007-08 certification), so treat cross-context transfer as a hypothesis; the badge/SDT/per-mechanic effects behind the recommendation are Western-by-inference.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/SYNTHESIS.md` (Feature 3 + `## Gaps & caveats`), `research/2026-07-16-indonesian-teacher-onboarding-literature/sources.md`; `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/coursera/screenshots/02-shareable-certificate.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/google-teacher-center/screenshots/01-certification-hero.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F1 + `## Peer Review`).

### Badge as in-loop participation recognition (not a terminal credential)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (literature-grounded)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F2) — on Duolingo, badges attach to **challenges inside the loop**, not to course completion ("Monthly challenges… Complete each month's challenge to earn exclusive badges"), sitting alongside quests and XP as ongoing recognition (`research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/03-quests-and-badges.png`). The literature grounds the role split: badges reliably boost activity via extrinsic motivation but do **not** raise intrinsic motivation [Dicheva et al. 2020], and as a *completion* driver they are fragile — teacher-candidate badges worth 1–2% of grade saw only 33–43% complete the final badging despite 68–97% doing the practice; framed as **participation/recognition** they sustain engagement where **competency** badges fail (of 30 funded efforts, 4 of 5 "participation" badges built thriving ecosystems, 0 "competency" badges did [Hickey & Chartrand 2020]).
- **When it works:** Badges are the right tool for "keep you engaged this week," not "prove you are qualified." Their strength is *frequency and low stakes*, which supports the participation/relatedness levers gamification actually moves — so scope them to in-session/between-session recognition (streaks of activity, monthly challenges, "helped 3 peers"), paired with a real terminal credential (see *Institution-recognized credentials*) that carries the completion job. The reconciling variable across all badge evidence is **perceived value**: a participation-framed badge that signals belonging outperforms a competency-framed one that over-promises a credential it cannot back.
- **When it backfires:** A badge as the **sole terminal reward** for a credential-signalling audience collapses completion (the 33–43% failure mode) — if badge-gated completion drops, the badge lacks perceived value and is doing the certificate's job badly. The completion-collapse figure is **one US teacher-candidate study (n=151) with a 1–2%-of-grade incentive confound, applied to Indonesian teachers by inference**; the participation-vs-competency effect is Western/general. Treat "badges sustain engagement here" as a hypothesis to validate locally, and never sell a badge as proof of competency.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/03-quests-and-badges.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F2 + `## Peer Review`).

### Tiered / leveled credential (progression hook on a terminal credential)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed; **untested design hypothesis** (no external citation)
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F3) — Google for Education presents two tiers side by side, "Google Certified Educator **Level 1**" ("Demonstrate your mastery") and **Level 2** ("Validate your expertise and advanced skills"), each "Valid for 3 years | Free of charge" with a "Get certified → Prepare by…" checklist (`research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/google-teacher-center/screenshots/02-tiered-free-credentials.png`). Note this is a *free exam-based competency micro-credential*, distinct from a paid completion certificate.
- **When it works:** Splitting a credential into ordered levels lets it keep its extrinsic **completion** value (the reason to finish Level 1) while borrowing a *levels/progression* mechanic (a reason to come **back** for Level 2) — one mechanic serving both completion and retention where a single one-shot certificate has no return hook. Making it **free** matters for a low-and-middle-income audience: the value is the signal, not scarcity.
- **When it backfires:** This is a **benchmark design hypothesis observed on one platform, with no supporting literature** (`references.md` has no "levels raise retention" source) — the "how to validate" (single certificate vs. Level 1 → Level 2 ladder on *return rate*) is the primary evidence path, not confirmation. Do not present tiered credentials as a proven retention lever; a poorly-differentiated Level 2 (no real added signalling value) gives no reason to climb.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/google-teacher-center/screenshots/02-tiered-free-credentials.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F3 + `## Peer Review`).

### Progress bar / goal-gradient as the low-risk default motivation mechanic
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F4) — Duolingo shows a Daily Quest "Earn 10 XP" with a 0/10 progress bar on both dashboard and quests screen, rendering near-term advancement legible at a glance (`research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/02-gamification-dashboard.png`). (Goal-gradient/endowed-progress also appears in *Bounded, reversible onboarding momentum scaffolding* and underlies *Post-task reward*; this entry names it as a *standalone motivation mechanic* with an audience-fit rationale.)
- **When it works:** A visible bar toward a small near-term goal exploits the goal-gradient effect (visible advancement increases effort and completion [Kivetz et al.]) and supports **autonomy** with no social comparison. For a low-connectivity, collectivist, credential-driven audience it is the **safest, highest-priority** simple mechanic in the study's reasoned order: it needs no persistent connectivity or account, cannot expose PII, carries no demotivation tail, and *amplifies* the terminal credential (progress toward the certificate) rather than competing with it. Reach for it first when the riskier social/competitive mechanics (leaderboards) are a poor cultural fit.
- **When it backfires:** The audience-fit ranking (F4–F8) is **designer judgment over lever evidence, not a measured ranking** — there is no isolated per-mechanic teacher/Indonesia study — so treat "progress bar is highest-priority here" as a hypothesis. Avoid over-claiming a *competence* benefit (the study dropped that framing to stay consistent with the ETR&D meta it cites); rest the case on autonomy + goal-gradient. A progress bar tied to *activity/completion* is not a mastery signal (see *Mastery signal tied to accuracy*) — do not let it certify learning it does not measure.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/platforms/duolingo/screenshots/02-gamification-dashboard.png`, `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F4 + `## Peer Review`).

### Prosocial / collective framing as a completion lever for collectivist audiences
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed (literature-grounded); cross-cutting framing lever
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification` (F9) — a *framing* lever applied across all the mechanics rather than a captured UI: across four PD MOOCs, **prosocially-motivated educators were more likely to complete** while intrinsically-motivated ones completed *less* [Anghel et al. 2024], aligning with Indonesia's low-individualism culture (Hofstede IDV=14) and its native collaborative PD structure (MGMP subject-teacher working groups). Honest limit: the benchmark captured only *individual* mechanics (XP, streaks, individual leaderboard), so this lever is drawn from literature + context, not from an observed UI.
- **When it works:** "Design for intrinsic motivation" and "design for **completion**" are not the same goal; prosocial framing is the one lever the evidence suggests bridges them for a collectivist professional audience. Framing goals and rewards around serving others ("practise today to help your students / your school") plausibly outperforms personal-achievement framing, and it converts the riskiest mechanic — individual leaderboards — into a fit one (cohort/school shared goals; see *Time-boxed peer league*). Generalizes to any collectivist / relatedness-driven audience where personal-achievement framing under-motivates.
- **When it backfires:** The prosocial-completion effect is from PD MOOCs (not Indonesia-specific) and the cultural fit is inferred from Hofstede IDV=14, so it is a **hypothesis to A/B locally** (prosocial vs. personal-achievement framing on completion/retention), not a settled result. Prosocial framing that rings hollow (guilt-tripping, or "for your students" over a feature that plainly serves the company) backfires like any inauthentic copy. It reframes, it does not replace, the underlying mechanic's own risks.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/SYNTHESIS.md` (F9 + `## Peer Review`), `research/2026-07-17-certificate-vs-badge-gamification/references.md`.

## Re-engagement & return

### AI-generated session recap on return (rebuild context, force light retrieval)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience` ("Review what you learned to go faster today" → "Your recent learnings": an AI summary of the last session with key points, a code example, and "Was this useful? Yes/No" — `research/2026-07-03-datacamp-learning-experience/platforms/datacamp/screenshots/02-recap-prompt.png`, `03-recap-your-recent-learnings.png`).
- **When it works:** Multi-session learning has a re-entry cost and a forgetting curve; a recap that rebuilds context and forces light retrieval before continuing lowers the cost of resuming and answers "why is it worth picking this back up?"
- **When it backfires:** It is a recurring-LLM-inference surface (OPEX + latency + hallucination/eval risk) that must be grounded to the learner's *actual* work; it is arguably a learning-effectiveness feature mis-filed under motivation. Fund it as part of a shared, grounded AI pipeline, not a bespoke second AI build.

### Asynchronous "someone acted on your work" return trigger
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (community corrections arrive asynchronously on your submitted writing/speaking, giving a concrete reason to return — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/13-community-give-correction-feed.png`).

## Social & human-in-the-loop learning

### Peer/community correction of real output (reciprocity loop)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience` (learners submit short writing/speaking and get corrected by other members framed as native speakers, and correct others in return — Discover feed + one-tap "Give correction" — `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/12-community-hub-native-speakers.png`, `13-community-give-correction-feed.png`, `14-give-correction-view.png`); `research/2026-07-09-benchmark-synthesis-learn-to-hire-loop` (Pattern 6 notes it as promising but with the receiving side and correction quality unobserved).
- **When it works:** The hardest, most-avoided part of learning is producing real output and finding out where it's wrong; routing production to human correctors approximates individualised feedback that automated grading cannot, at community scale and near-zero marginal cost. The reciprocity loop (correct others to earn corrections) supplies the correction labour *and* builds a social obligation that pulls learners back.
- **When it backfires:** It is a two-sided marketplace with cold-start liquidity risk — the make-or-break metric is submission→first-correction time; if corrections are slow or scarce, the whole feature fails. It also stacks a UGC moderation/safety surface (often minors) and can trigger exposure anxiety that suppresses beginner participation. In the study only the *give-correction* side was observed; the receiving side and the "native speaker" quality claim were not, so treat the value as unproven until the second side is instrumented.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/screenshots/14-give-correction-view.png`, `research/2026-07-03-busuu-learning-experience/platforms/busuu/notes.md`.

## Efficacy measurement

### Mastery-accrual-from-zero without a pre/post baseline (a differentiation gap)
- **Kind:** benchmark-observed
- **Where seen:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops` (every skill starts at "Not started"; improvement is shown as mastery accrued from zero, with *no forced pre/post diagnostic*, so the platform reports coverage + proficiency, not a normalized learning gain — `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-course-overview.png`).
- **When it works:** Starting from zero and letting learners optionally test out is low-friction and motivating for a broad audience — good for activation and course coverage.
- **When it backfires:** Coverage/proficiency is *not* a measured learning gain (which efficacy research treats as the credible proof of upskilling), and platform mastery is near-transfer, not demonstrated far transfer. This is recorded here as the clearest **opening for our product**: a lightweight pre/post diagnostic that reports a real, normalized gain would let us claim efficacy the benchmarked platforms cannot — but it must be de-risked first, because an upfront diagnostic adds onboarding friction that could be fatal to activation (prototype the friction before building any psychometrics).
- **⚠️ Tension / cross-reference (flagged 2026-07-13):** the onboarding-activation study (`research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps`) supplies direct evidence *for* this caveat: its headline finding is "prove value before the wall," and its cold-gate anti-pattern (CodeSignal demanding a full account form, and its assessment, before any value — `research/2026-07-13-onboarding-activation-education-apps/platforms/codesignal/screenshots/01-signup-gate-create-account.png`) shows a strong assessment/placement mechanic wasted when placed *before* the first win. An upfront efficacy diagnostic is the same shape of pre-win friction. This does not flip the guidance (a pre/post diagnostic is still the efficacy opening) but hardens the sequencing rule: if we run a baseline diagnostic, deliver a win first or fold the diagnostic *into* the first task (assessment-as-onboarding, see the onboarding section), rather than gating value behind it.
- **Evidence:** `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/platforms/khan-academy/screenshots/01-course-overview.png`, `research/2026-07-06-benchmark-learning-effectiveness-loops/SYNTHESIS.md` (Feature 7).
