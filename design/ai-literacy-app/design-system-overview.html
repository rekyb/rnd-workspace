The overrides

Superseded — Foundations, locked decisions

“Brand direction: Calm & credible — deep-teal primary”
“Primary hue: Deep, serious teal #0F766E”

v3 moves the brand to indigo velvet. A change of brand thesis, not a recolour, overturning a locked foundations decision. It gives the strongest brand contrast the system has had — --primary carries white text at 10.18:1 (teal was 5.47, green 8.47).

Four consequences, forced by the semantic model rather than chosen:

  • The warm ramp is not the meaning colours. It lands on top of win and danger, and the three warms collapse into each other under colour-blindness. --win-fill takes amber-flame only; tiger-orange and cayenne-red become illustration-only --deco-* tokens. See below.
  • danger stays a true red. The palette’s cayenne fails AA body as text (3.17:1) and is the weaker signal. This one does not bend to the palette.
  • safe keeps the v1 teal — verified clear of both --verify-text (dE 97.0) and the indigo brand (dE 74.9).
  • readiness is deep forest green — dE 83.1 from the brand. Green reads complete/go, and safe has vacated green.

Palette-native: #3D348B #7678ED #F7B801 #F18701 #F35B04. A 5-colour ramp cannot fill a theme-aware system, so pressed states, tints, and the readiness pair are derived or carried forward — tokens.css marks which is which.

Superseded — Foundations §5

“Frame the credential in terms teachers know: ‘Sertifikat + Jam PKB’”

Foundations is dated 2026-07-15; the certificate-vs-badge decision resolved 2026-07-17. That framing is not permitted in v1. No self-issued certificate, and no certification, PD-hour, PKB, or government-recognition claim anywhere in the product. Badges are in-loop recognition; the terminal reward is a portfolio-worthy readiness completion, designed PD-credit-ready rather than PD-credit-claiming.

Everything else in foundations stands unchanged and is authoritative.

The warm ramp, and why it isn’t the meaning colours

The palette supplies amber → orange → red. That ramp lands exactly on top of win and danger — and only one of the three can be trusted with a signal.

--win-fill#F7B801 amber-flamePromoted to the win signal
--deco-orange#F18701 tiger-orangeIllustration only
--deco-red#F35B04 cayenne-redIllustration only

To a person with red-green colour deficiency — about 8% of men — the three warms are effectively one colour. Simulated with the Viénot model, measured in Delta-E:

PairNormalDeuteranopiaProtanopia
amber-flame vs tiger-orange27.38.37.6
tiger-orange vs cayenne-red23.87.76.0

So mapping win→amber-flame and danger→cayenne would have halved the win/danger separation for those users — on the signal that carries the student-data guardrail. #F35B04 also fails AA body as text (3.17:1). danger therefore keeps its true red, and the swap we did make improves things:

win vs dangerNormalDeuteranopiaProtanopia
old #F59E0B vs #B91C1C58.423.726.1
new #F7B801 vs #B91C1C73.126.329.2

Tiger-orange and cayenne-red are never used for buttons, callouts, states, or meaning-bearing icons. They live in illustration and examples — which is where foundations §7 says local character belongs anyway. In an illustration their mutual confusability is harmless; on a control it would corrupt the signal.

Meaning colours

Semantic, not decorative. Each teaches exactly one thing: amber never appears except on a win, red never except on a never-do. Consistent use is what makes a colour a learnable signal.

Shown as live callouts rather than bare swatches — deliberately. A naked amber chip would break the rule this section exists to document: a meaning colour never appears without an icon and a label beside it.

Selalu verifikasi sebelum dipakai. --verify-bg / --verify-text · the check-it habit
Prompt ini aman — tidak ada data siswa. --safe-bg / --safe-text · privacy-safe confirmation · moved off green in v2

Readiness

The one place the design system encodes the certificate-vs-badge decision. Badges keep the amber beat; the terminal completion gets a ground of its own — deep forest green, measuring dE 83.1 from the indigo brand and clearly distinct from every other claimed colour. Green reads complete/go, and safe has vacated green, so the slot is free.

The mark is a light green, not amber — deliberately. An amber mark would read as a win and muddy the exact distinction this token exists to make. The brand’s own slate-blue was ruled out too: #7678ED on this ground is 2.29:1, a hard fail.

How grounds get picked. Every candidate is tested with Delta-E against every claimed colour — contrast ratio is the wrong tool, since all dark colours score ≈1.2:1 against each other regardless of hue. That method has killed, across revisions: violet (near-complementary to a green brand), oxblood #4A1520 (dE 8.1 from danger-bg), cool slate #171B24 (dE 8.7 from ink — reads “screen went dark”), and espresso (dE 9.5 from ink).

The distinction is composition, not just hue. This is the only surface in the product where a colour fills the entire viewport. A badge is a chip inside a screen; this is the screen. Below is the only full-bleed colour block on this page, mirroring that rule.

Indigo is the strongest brand contrast the system has had. on-primary / primary in light: teal 5.47 → green 8.47 → indigo 10.18:1.

But the tightest pair has moved into dark mode. It used to be win-text / surface at 4.78:1 in light; it is now on-primary / primary at 4.73:1 in dark, because the slate-blue has to carry the chrome there and only just clears the AA floor. That is the pair to watch on a real dark screen — and the reason dark, not light, is now the tighter theme.

PatternThe rule that matters most
Path node6 types × 3 states. A locked node always explains why and deep-links to the exact check. Instructional, never punitive.
Prompt cardAccess (open / learn-first / unlocked) × save — two orthogonal dimensions, not four flat states. unlocked decays to open so the dictionary doesn’t feel more gated over time.
Prompt detailBuat di aplikasi is a dormant slot. The prompt-only path must stay complete — generation-ready, not generation-dependent.
Locked explainerNames the exact node and deep-links to it. “Go learn more” is a failure. Uses the verify register, not danger — being gated is not a violation.

Readiness completionThe only full-viewport colour in the product. No certificate iconography, ever.

Feedback panelCorrect answer → why it matters for practice → what next. A wrong answer is not a never-do and must not borrow the danger register.
Onboarding stepOne decision per screen. Completes fully offline; nothing leaves the device until the account moment.
Account momentLoss-framed, never blocks. Protects real professional progress — never “save your badge”.

Open questions

Recorded, not resolved. Ordered by risk.

  1. The highest-risk assumption. Whether a readiness completion reads as credible to an Indonesian teacher without certificate iconography is untested — and it stacks directly on the cert-vs-badge decision’s own flagged weakness (only 2 directly-Indonesian anchors). If teachers read the no-certificate screen as worthless rather than honest, the terminal motivation collapses and the badge-demotion premise needs revisiting. First in line for primary research.
  2. Anda vs kamu. Teacher-as-learner argues for kamu (Duolingo’s register, which onboarding copies); teacher-as-professional argues for Anda. Not settled by desk research. Settle it with teachers.
  3. Hex values on real devices. Contrast is computed, not device-verified. Test at 360px, one-handed, on a real mid/low-end Android in bright daylight.
  4. Does unlocked decaying to open hold up? It keeps the dictionary from feeling more gated over time — but teachers may want durable proof of what they earned.
  5. The brand has changed three times in one day — teal → green → indigo — and none of the three has been shown to an Indonesian teacher. Brand hue is one of the cheapest things to test and one of the most expensive to get wrong after launch. The palette is not the risk; the absence of a read from a real teacher is.
  6. Two of the five palette colours never appear in the UI. --deco-orange and --deco-red are illustration-only, because they can’t be told apart from --win-fill under colour-blindness. If the warm ramp was meant to be the brand’s visible signature rather than its illustration palette, that intent is not currently expressed — and it can’t be without weakening the win/danger signal.
  7. Dark mode flattens the brand hierarchy. --primary and --primary-bright converge there, so shape and weight carry a distinction colour carries in light. Combined with the 4.73:1 tightest pair, dark mode is where this brand is under most strain.
  8. Indigo’s local reading is untested. Teal carried calm/credible; green carried marketplace. Indigo is close to the batik-indigo tradition, which may read as locally rooted — or as generic-tech. Unknown either way without a teacher.
  9. Bahasa copy and term choices need a native-speaker educator review — including whether to keep prompt as a loanword or gloss it.
